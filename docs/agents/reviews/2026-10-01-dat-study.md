## The answer

**No: close it.** The MSTR premium is not an arbitrage, and it did not predict MSTR against bitcoin. There is no
creation or redemption, and in 2020-2024 the premium's pull back toward its mean came almost entirely from the company
selling shares at a premium (84 % of it over 20 days, all of it over 60), not from the price. The bias-corrected
slopes are zero or the wrong sign (bootstrap p 0.36-0.78), and every tradable form lost to its benchmark. For this
account the trades are not reachable as designed, and none could be confirmed if they were. It cannot short (no CFD
account; crypto derivatives are banned for UK retail). Its ISA cannot buy a bitcoin ETN after 2026-04-06. A held-out
test would have 0.09 power at a large effect (20 years forward for 0.8, MSTR; about 5 for BMNR). And the 13 measurable
2025-2026 "discount-closing" announcements by treasury companies trailed their own coin by 24 % (log) on average over
60 days.

## 1. Why mNAV is not arbitrage-bounded

* **No creation or redemption.** An ETF's price is tied to its holdings because an authorised participant can swap one
  for the other. A treasury company has no such channel. Holders cannot redeem shares for coins, and nobody can
  deliver coins for new shares at NAV. The premium closes only when the company acts. It issues shares or converts at a
  premium and buys coins, which lowers mNAV by construction. Or, at a discount, it buys back stock, sells coins, makes a
  tender, merges or liquidates. Otherwise investors simply change their minds.
* **What the closed-end-fund literature says.** Discounts move with sentiment and persist (Lee, Shleifer & Thaler,
  JF 1991). They are widest where holding the arbitrage is costly (Pontiff, QJE 1996). Arbitrage needs capital and fails
  when prices run away (Shleifer & Vishny, JF 1997). The event that closes a gap is a structural one: activist
  open-ending attempts cut discounts to about half on average (Bradley, Brav, Goldstein & Jiang, JFE 2010). GBTC's
  discount of about 48-50 % in December 2022 went to zero the day it converted to an ETF with creation and redemption
  (2024-01-11; CoinDesk).
* **How MSTR's premium actually closed (screen, descriptive).** Over h days, the change in log mNAV is MSTR's return
  against BTC plus an issuance term, Δlog shares − Δlog coins. Issuance at a premium makes that term negative. Regressed
  on log mNAV (`results/screen.json`, `decomposition_x`):

  | Horizon | Δ log mNAV on log mNAV | of which issuance | of which price (an investor's return) |
  |---|---:|---:|---:|
  | 20 days | −0.177 | −0.149 | −0.028 |
  | 60 days | −0.323 | −0.341 | +0.019 |

  When the premium was above its median, the 60-day issuance term averaged −0.225 (log). Below the median it averaged
  −0.0004. The premium did come back toward its mean, but through the company's share sales, which raised bitcoin per
  share. MSTR's price relative to BTC did not do the work. Choueifaty, Froidure & Cabrol (SSRN 5172347, 2025) reach the
  same point: in 2021-2024 MSTR rose 7.5× against BTC's 3.2× "despite a stable Ratio Premium".
* **The textbook trade, as it happened (screen, descriptive, `results/episodes.json`).** Kerrisdale published "Long
  BTC / Short MicroStrategy" on 2024-03-28. It cited about 2.57×, a diluted count; this study's basic-share measure read
  2.22 simple and 2.46 EV-based the previous close. Equal-notional pair from that day's open:
  * by the premium's peak (2024-11-20, simple mNAV 3.40), MSTR +144 % and BTC +33 %, so the pair lost 111 % of
    notional. A 20 %-margin CFD short is closed out long before that;
  * by 2024-12-31, with mNAV at 1.70, below the entry level, the pair was still −17.5 %. Issuance had raised bitcoin
    per share, and MSTR's daily beta to BTC was 1.17.
* **What "arbitrage" can mean for an account that cannot short.** Three things, and none is an arbitrage:
  * (a) switch between MSTR and bitcoin on the premium (H2);
  * (b) stay out of MSTR when the premium is high;
  * (c) buy a discounted treasury company when it announces a closing action (§7).

  Each carries the coin's risk unhedged and the premium's risk on top. The only short-MSTR instrument a long-only
  account can hold is Leverage Shares' −3× daily ETP (SMST, LSE, GBp). It loses about (k² − k)σ²/2 = 6σ² a year in log
  terms to daily rebalancing, about 5.6 a year at MSTR's screen volatility of 96 %, so it is an instrument for days,
  not a hedge.

## 5. Power (inference; `power.py` → `results/power.json`)

Monte Carlo of the Stambaugh model at the screen's own persistence, noise and shock correlation, with the critical value
from the null. **Large** = one screen-SD of log mNAV moves the 60-day relative return by 10 % (scaled to h). **Moderate**
= 5 %.

| Sample | n (h = 20 / 60) | Large | Moderate | The screen's own corrected effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Held-out, 2025-01-02 → 2026-09-30 (437 days) | 21 / 7 | 0.09 / 0.09 (EV 0.11 / 0.11) | 0.07 / 0.08 | 0.05 / 0.03 (wrong sign) |
| Forward 5 years | 63 / 21 | 0.19 / 0.17 (EV 0.27 / 0.25) | 0.10 / 0.10 | — |
| Forward 10 years | 126 / 42 | 0.38 / 0.33 (EV 0.53 / 0.50) | 0.15 / 0.13 | — |
| Forward 20 years | 252 / 84 | 0.75 / 0.70 (EV 0.87 / 0.86) | 0.28 / 0.24 | — |

* **The bias swamps a large effect.** At the held-out size the bias alone is −0.151 (20 days) and −0.531 (60 days),
  against a large effect's −0.073 and −0.218.
* **H2's timing skill** (SE 26.4 bp a day over 977 days). An edge of +20 % a year over the static mix needs 31.8 bp a
  day of timing: held-out power 0.20, and 16.5 years to 0.8. +40 % a year: 0.49 and 4.1 years.
* **Power is under 0.5 for every economically meaningful effect** in the held-out years and in any forward test
  shorter than about 10 years (EV) or 20 (simple).
* **What was seen before.** A held-out "pass" would also be weak evidence. The drafter knows the premium compressed in
  2025 and that MSTR fell far more than BTC in the second half of 2025. Search snippets read during the accessibility
  research quoted MSTR's mNAV near 0.86 and 1.06 at unstated dates in 2025-2026. A fail there would be informative; a
  pass would not.

## 8. What would change the answer

* **A binding closing mechanism on a name this account can buy**, at a known price and date. For example a tender or a
  liquidation at NAV, or a conversion to a fund with creation and redemption (GBTC 2024-01-11). Then the gap is an
  event, not a forecast. Even then the coin's move is unhedged here.
* **A way to hedge the coin.** Crypto derivatives for UK retail are banned (FCA, 2025-08-01), so the hedge would have
  to be a short or inverse coin ETN listed in London. Whether one exists and is sold at Trading 212 was not checked.
* **Davies opening a CFD account** would make H3 reachable. But H3 lost on the screen and the Kerrisdale episode shows
