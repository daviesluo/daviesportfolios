## Verdict: yes, at LIVE's size, by a narrower margin than the books' own gap

- **Book for book, USDT-GBP is clearly and recently ahead of USDC-GBP** on LIVE's rules with the £1 holding line: every month
  since May at every size; since 09-01 by +21.0 % a year at £600 (P(≤ 0) 0.0004, 38 days ahead, 17 behind); in the last 30 days
  by +15.5 (P 0.019, 20 / 10). LIVE's own record agrees: its trips of £1 or more made 1.75 times as much on USDT-GBP as on
  USDC-GBP per pound of book (1.5 times without its best day), and USDT-GBP was ahead on 9 of the 13 days since 10-15 that
  differ.
- **All the capital on USDT-GBP beats LIVE as it runs (both books, half each)**, but by much less than that gap, because
  USDT-GBP's return per pound falls as its rungs double. At £600: **+7.1 % a year since 09-01 [+3.0, +11.3], P(≤ 0) 0.014**,
  34 days ahead and 20 behind; +31.7 out of sample (P 0.000); +3.6 in the last 30 days [−1.3, +8.2] (P 0.17). Ahead in every
  month since May at all three sizes; in October by 4.2 points at £600 (41.0 against 36.8 % a year).
- That meets the bar the holding-line verdict used (P(≤ 0) about 0.01 or less since September and out of sample, LIVE's own
  record agreeing, no mechanism downside) at £600 within rounding, less well at £1,200 (P 0.019) and not at £2,400 (P 0.051).
  **The last 30 days alone do not show it** (P 0.17–0.29): the case rests on the two months since the books tightened and on
  the history. It is positive in expectation with high but not complete confidence; the downside below is concentration, not
  a rule failure.

## 2. Size on one book: does doubling USDT-GBP's rungs dilute its fills?

- **In the replay, mildly:** since 09-01 USDT-GBP filled 5.73 pounds of entry a day per pound of rung at LIVE's £50 rungs (both
  books, £600), 5.33 at £100 (USDT alone at £600 or both at £1,200) and 4.72 at £200. A doubling costs about 7 % of the fills per
  pound; that is in every figure above.
- **The book is deep enough** (REVX-FAIR's recorded books, 2026-09-26 → 10-29, ~36,000 snapshots each): the median best bid and
  ask each hold about 5,000 coins on both books, and 9,600–12,900 within 10 bps of the mid on USDT-GBP; a £100 rung is about
  133 coins, under 3 % of the touch.
- **USDT-GBP trades more:** about £96,000 a day of prints against £62,000 on USDC-GBP; 98 % of the pounds in prints of £100 or
  more on both. Its median print is £0.10 (the minimum-size trader of §3 of the holding-line verdict), which the £1 line now
  ignores.

## 4. Downsides

- **Concentration.** One book and one coin: the asks' inventory (about half the capital) becomes all USDT, so a USDT-specific
  de-peg is the whole book's, where today it is half (the executor's 50 bps de-peg guard still withholds entries). Days are no
  smoother: USDT alone and both books have the same worst day in the replay (−0.23 % of capital).
- **The margin is narrowing.** October's is the smallest of six months, and the last 30 days alone are not significant.
- **The switch itself:** the USDC LIVE holds must be sold and USDT bought, maker only (Davies: "换币也用挂单不要有转换损失"); until
  that is done, USDT-GBP's asks are short of coin and quote less.
- No fill dilution beyond the replay's 7 % a doubling, and nothing in the executor's rules behaves differently on one book.
