- **(b) no 0.3 % rung, its money on the 0.1 / 0.2 % rungs: not positive.** At £600 (LIVE): −1.09 % a year since 09-01 (P(≤ 0)
  0.67, 32 days ahead and 20 behind), −1.94 out of sample (P 0.86), +2.79 over the last 30 days (P 0.12). The £100 / £200
  and £200 / £100 splits are no better since 09-01 (−2.05, −1.09); £200 / £100 loses −5.85 out of sample (P 1.00). At £1,200

  earns the most a trip (without it LIVE's bps a trip fall from 15.1 to 12.4 and its trips a day from 5.95 to 5.02).
- **(c) no 0.3 % + exit a tick beyond fair: positive, but not certain.** £600: +0.97 since 09-01 [80 % −1.90, +3.67],
  P 0.31; +3.60 out of sample (P 0.05); +6.31 over the last 30 days (P 0.000). The gain since 09-01 is smaller than (d)'s

- **(d) exit a tick beyond fair alone (`exitOffset` 1, variant-6's rule): basically certain positive.** £600: **+1.49 % a year

  over the last 30 days (P 0.002, 22 / 7).** £1,200: +1.66 (P 0.065), +3.74 (P 0.000), +3.21 (P 0.009). Positive in every
  window at both sizes; the one rule of the four that is.
- **The live twins cannot tell yet.** `n600no03` (variant-3: LIVE's rules less the 0.3 % rung) has one whole day, and on

## 3. What it means

For LIVE's USDT-GBP book at £600, keep the 0.3 % rung. The exit a tick beyond fair is the change the replay supports in
every window at both sizes, and variant-6 is already measuring it on LIVE's rules; the case for it on LIVE rests on its
pre-registered reading, Davies' call. Dropping the 0.3 % rung with x1 is worth watching through `n600no03` and variant-6
together, not worth acting on now. Nothing was built.
