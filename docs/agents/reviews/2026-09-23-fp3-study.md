## The answer

**Binance has one mechanism of its own that passes a pre-registered test, and it is not worth money: it
earned about what cash earns, nearly all of it (98 %) in 2023–24, and it has lost since Binance started
capping its own wicks.**

* **Liquidity for Binance's liquidation cascades (test CB) — passes its bar, fading, ≈ cash.** Resting
  bids 5 / 10 / 15 % under the last close on the ten most-traded Binance coins (point in time, delisted
  coins included), the take-profit at half the drop placed by the exchange the instant a bid fills (an
  OPO order list), history priced as the venue now is (every fill checked against Binance's new price
  range cap). Out of sample 2023-01 → 2026-09: **+$488.99 on the $3,000 it locks, 462 round trips, 89 %
  won — 4.4 % a year**, above the random-time null's 95th percentile (−$41.56), positive with doubled
  costs (+$393.92), no month above 18 %. All six conditions hold. But: 2023–24 made +$477.94 and
  2025-01 → 2026-09 made **+$11.05**; **since the price range rule (2026-04-01 → 09-21) it lost $108.98 on
  60 trips**; most of the profit is the rebound after a crash rather than the wick price (entering at the
  fill minute's close instead keeps +$399); and the one subset that held up everywhere, the seven majors,
  was picked after the result and earns 3.3 % a year — below cash. Plan with roughly zero. If a row of
  Binance's own is wanted on the page, CB is the only candidate with a pass; on paper it would measure the
  capped venue at about ten fills a month across ten coins (60 in the 5.7 months since the rule), never
  money on this evidence.
* **Binance's zero-fee stablecoin books (test ZF) — free, real, and too small.** Binance charges 0 % maker
  AND taker on ten stablecoin books "until further notice" (public announcements; the UK is excluded only
  from the two USD fiat books). Resting 0 % quotes 2 / 5 / 10 bps around fair, filled only by prints strictly
  through: on the six USD books **+$37.18 out of sample on $3,600 locked — 1.4 % a year** (it passes the
  other five conditions); EURI's books, PR5's mechanism on Binance, lost $439 in sample and fail the
  stress and the one-month test; the pooled test fails. The books are one tick wide and a once-a-minute
  quote earns about 2 bps a few times a day. At 10 bps a fill everything loses.
* **Binance's calendar pays the other side.** Buying an hour after "Binance Will Delist…" and selling a
  day before trading stops (test DL): **−$2,689.89 over 111 out-of-sample events of $100, median event
  −31 %, 10 of 111 positive** — forced holders are not overshooting, the fall continues. New listings
  fall (median −28 % over 30 days from the first close, 25 % positive). Launchpool on stablecoins pays
  0.04–0.30 % a project, under 1.5 % a year. Nothing a long-only UK spot account can harvest.
* **Revolut X: nothing new.** Its gold-token books are the one new place a once-a-minute 0 % quote could
  sit (PAXG and XAUT, 10–11 bps wide), and they fail the same inequality as fp2's (h/σ₁ₘ 1.4 on weekdays)
  with about $34 k a day between the four books. PR5 (on paper) stays the only mechanism.
* **The combination is routing and protection, not return**: GBP enters through Revolut X (PR5's
  stablecoin/GBP quotes), dollars live on Binance, where U/USDT and U/USDC swap USDT and USDC at 0 % (the zero-fee USD
  fiat books exclude UK residents); and a
  cascade on Binance meets Revolut X's lagging UK bids (fp2), a protective overlay once a year.

**The finding that matters beyond this search: since 2026-03-09 → ~03-30 Binance caps every trade to
`referencePrice × (1 ± R)`**, the reference being the mean trade price of the last 300 s, R = 15 % on
BTC, ETH, BNB, XRP, TRX, DOGE, SOL, 25 % on 428 other USDT pairs, 2 % on the USDC, FDUSD and U stablecoin
books, 5 % on USDE (public: `GET https://www.binance.com/api/v3/executionRules`, `…/referencePrice`,
`…/referencePrice/calculation`; the `data-api.binance.vision` mirror does not serve them, `api.binance.com`
refuses this machine). A taker order that would trade beyond it is EXPIRED
(`EXECUTION_RULE_PRICE_RANGE_EXCEEDED`). On 17 coins, 1-minute lows more than 15 % under the previous
five minutes' mean fell from 2.39 to 0.07 per 100,000 coin-minutes, and beyond 25 % from 1.63 to 0. It
means: PR2's 2 % rungs on USDC/FDUSD can never fill again, USDE cannot print 0.65 again, and every
wick-based backtest before March 2026 prices a tail the venue has removed. It also means a Binance stop
sold at market in a crash can come back EXPIRED instead of filled — the Binance paper venue should expect
that reason before any Binance order is ever live.

Three facts shape everything below:

* **The fee is the wall, and Binance itself removes it on ten books.** At 10 bps a side a round trip costs
  20 bps plus the spread; every Binance quoting idea of the earlier programmes died on it. The zero-fee
  promotions are the only place a Binance resting order is free, so the zero-fee books are where a
  Binance-own quoting edge would have to live.
* **The venue now caps its own wicks.** Measured on 17 coins' 1-minute klines (`results/m_price_range_wicks.json`):
  before the rollout, 2.39 minutes per 100,000 coin-minutes had a low more than 15 % below the previous five
  closes' mean and 1.63 more than 25 %; after it, 0.07 and 0.00 — the deepest post-rollout wick is 23.7 %
  (CELO), inside its 25 % cap. The tail beyond the cap was one day's (2025-10-10: 235 of the pre-rollout
  minutes beyond 5 %). Leaving that day out, wicks beyond 5 % halved (3.56 → 1.82 per 100,000) and those
  beyond 10 % did not fall (0.12 → 0.58): the rule removes the 2025-10-10 kind of tail, not the ordinary
  wick — and with the tail goes most of what the first two programmes' wick ideas (PR2, T3) and any crash
  ladder would have earned in history.
* **Binance's order budget is not binding** (200,000 a day), unlike Revolut X's 1,000; what binds a
  once-a-minute quoter on Binance is the fee and the one-minute staleness, not the placements.

## 5. Where this programme differs from the first two, and why

1. **The fee was never the whole wall.** The first two programmes killed Binance quoting on its 10 bps fee
   and listed zero-fee pairs as "needs a signed call". The promotions are public (which books, since when,
   who is excluded); only this account's rate needs the key. With the fee at zero, the USD books still earn
   1.4 % a year: the binding constraint is the tick and HFT competition at the peg, not the fee.
2. **The venue changed under the wick ideas.** PR2 (stablecoin rungs to 2 %), T3 (LST rungs) and any crash
   ladder were priced on history in which Binance let trades run 60–99 % through the book. Since March 2026
   it caps them at 2–30 % of a 5-minute mean. PR2's 2 % rung on USDC/FDUSD can no longer fill; CB, priced with
   the cap, still passes on 2023–24 and loses since the cap arrived.
3. **fp2's crash ladder was on the wrong venue.** It died because Revolut X's UK book does not wick; the
   Binance version (CB) is where the forced selling lands. It passes its bar, but the money was in the
   years before the cap.
4. **fp2 left idea 30 (a de-peg lottery) untestable and did not look at the event calendar.** The
   delisting catalogue is public to the millisecond: forced holders do not overshoot (DL, −31 % median).
5. **fp1 read Binance as "0.10 %, no GBP, a price reference and breadth".** Add: 0 % on ten stablecoin
   books, 200,000 orders a day, exchange-placed exits (OPO), a public price-range cap, and public
   listing/delisting/Launchpool data — and none of it produces an edge above cash for a long-only UK spot
   account today.
6. **Same verdict as §3.23–§3.28, reached a third way**: what differs between the venues is cost, reach and
   now protection, not a rule. The one mechanism of Binance's own that passed (CB) did so on money earned
   before the venue started doing its job for it.

## 7. Checks

* **Order of work.** Each pre-registration was frozen before its data was pulled (ZF 15:52:41Z; aggTrades
  pulled 15:55–16:04. CB 15:54:23Z; its 1-minute days pulled 15:57–16:18; the hourly klines it screens on
  were on disk, unread. DL 15:56:56Z; its hourly klines pulled 15:57). The first result files are
  timestamped after the freezes. The disclosures in each file list what had been seen.
* **Determinism.** Two runs each, identical JSON: ZF `37ec1bdf…`, CB `103a3eae…`, DL `84883e09…`
  (`results/*_runs.sha256`).
* **Fills are prints.** ZF: 20 trips on four books checked against the per-minute print levels — every
  entry and every maker exit has a print strictly through its price, and the fair recomputed from scratch
  equals the simulator's (`results/zf_check_trips.json`). CB: 25 trips re-derived from the raw klines —
  bid from the close two minutes earlier, low strictly below, cap satisfied, target first exceeded in the
  exit minute or time stop at the 24 h close, P&L to the cent (`results/cb_check_trips.json`; the JSON rounds
  prices to 10 decimals, so PEPE-scale prices are compared at 1e-5 relative).
* **The screen drops nothing.** CB's rule run over four FULL months (SOL 2024-08, DOGE 2024-03, PEPE 2024-03,
  BANK 2026-07): 61 candidate fill minutes, none on an unscreened day, every simulated trip among them
  (`results/cb_check_screen.json`).
* **Too good? None is.** The one pass is 4.4 % a year. Its biggest risks are named: survivorship (the
  universe includes LUNA and FTT: −$182 in sample), look-ahead (universe from the previous day's volumes;
  bids from data two minutes old; targets from the next minute), the cap approximated from 1-minute closes
  (the no-cap arm differs by $22 over OOS), and an hourly null (weaker than a 1-minute one; the close twin
  is the sharper comparison and keeps 82 %).
* **Deviations from the pre-registrations, all disclosed, none changing a verdict.** (1) ZF's stress arm:
  as written ("at least one tick beyond") it equals the primary on a tick grid; the judged stress asks one
  tick more, and the literal reading is reported beside it (§4). (2) ZF's null draws minutes with a print
  AND a fair (the exit rule needs one); the text says "a minute with a print". (3) CB's half-spread `h_s`
  comes from ONE all-symbol bookTicker snapshot taken after the freeze, not a median of samples; `h_s` is
  0.001–24 bps across the 84 symbols still listed (5 bps for the 16 that are not) and enters only the
  time-stop exits (57 of 462 OOS). (4) CB treats a hole in the minute data as the
  data's end: 5 in-sample exits (three LUNA trips at the May 2022 halt, −$100 each, one WIN, one SHIB),
  none out of sample. (5) DL reads a pair's cease time as its last hourly bar within two days of the
  announced date, so a later relisting under the same symbol cannot move the exit.
* **Units.** ZF's EUR/EURI P&L is in EURI converted at EURUSDT; U and RLUSD counted at 1.0. DL is USDT on
  $100 notional.
* **Rate limits.** No Revolut X request was made by this programme (the gold facts come from fp2's
  snapshot; the shared lock was not needed). Binance: the bulk archive and `data-api.binance.vision`,
  paced; `www.binance.com` bapi at ≥ 1 s between requests. Live sampling: about 2 minutes of bookTicker.
