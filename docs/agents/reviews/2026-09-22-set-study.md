# The set study — four windows, three tapes, and four settled questions re-asked

Script `supabase/functions/agents/backtest_set2.ts`; raw output
`docs/agents/backtests/set2.json`. It re-asks the four choices that define the
live row — **which coins**, **equal slots**, **the mechanics**, **the venue** —
on evidence that did not exist when any of them was settled: **four** windows
instead of two (§3.15), and a **second** price series (§3.16), plus a **third**
on the one window that has one.

It invents no rule and proposes no row. It re-runs decisions that were already
made, so the prior is that nothing changes and the multiple-comparisons control
is what defends that prior. Every search states its arm count and its null
exactly.

**No wall clock is written into the output.** Re-running over the same four data
directories reproduces `set2.json` byte for byte; verified by two consecutive
runs. The run is identified by the SHA-256 of `backtest.ts`,
`31d27c7d82f8a94c…`, which is an input to this study and the same hash §3.15 and
§3.16 recorded.

---

## 0. What was asked, and the short answers

| | question | answer |
|---|---|---|
| **A1** | does the coin set change? | **No change.** 25 arms (5 drops, 20 adds); 3 improve the worst window in all four conditions where chance gives **1.56 to 12.5**. **Zero** add candidates are admitted by §4.15's bar. |
| **A2** | does anything beat equal slots on four windows and both tapes? | **Equal slots stands.** 2 of 6 arms beat it on the worst-of-four — and **0 of 6 beat it in the bear year on ANY of the three tapes**, the venue's own book included. The new windows-cleared arm fails, and fails with look-ahead too. |
| **A3** | do the cooldown, all-in/all-out or the stop surface look different once window D is in the ranking? | **The ranking moves almost completely and the settings do not change.** 42 arms; 9 beat the shipped setting on the worst-of-four, **0 of 42 beat it on every window**. Every winner buys window D by giving back A or B. |
| **A4** | any coin where Kraken beats Revolut X on the worst window? | **No, and the question should stop being asked.** 3 of 372 cells, 1 coin in 1 of 4 conditions — and that one is under the stop rule the loop no longer runs. 0 of 50 cells on the venue's own book. The worst Revolut X round trip (53.8 bps) is still cheaper than the best Kraken one (80.0 bps), so this is structural. |
| **A5** | the set that follows | **Identical to what runs today**, field for field. The margins are in §7. |

The one-line version: **this study changes nothing, and that is the result.**

## 1. The harness, and the proof it is the same arithmetic

Everything that costs money is **imported** from `backtest.ts`: `run`,
`resample`, `COSTS`, `SHIPPED_STOPS`, `stopsForKind`, `spreadOf`, and the live
rulebooks in `_shared/agents_strategy.ts`.

**One copy exists**, `runSet`, taken from `run` line by line. It adds exactly
four things, each of them something a question above varies:

- a mark at **every** bar plus the traded weight and an open flag (`run` samples
  every sixth bar, and which bar that is moves when the array start moves —
  §3.16's own reason for `runMarked`);
- a per-entry **weight** ∈ (0, 1] with the rest left in cash, and the entry ATR
  and binding stop distance A2's volatility and risk arms size on;
- **tranches** — entering in pieces, pyramiding, a partial profit target
  (A3's sizing question), copied from `backtest_execution.ts`'s `runExec`;
- a **nullable floor** and a **time stop**, neither of which `StopParams`
  carries (A3's stop surface).

Its fidelity is reported three ways, because a study whose only check is the
null branch has not checked the branch it uses:

| check | cells | worst \|Δreturn\| | worst \|Δdrawdown\| | worst \|Δtrades\| |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `runSet` null branch vs `run` | 844 | **0** | **0** | **0** |
| `runSet` with a weight that always returns 1 | 844 | **0** | **0** | **0** |
| `runSet` with a one-tranche plan | 844 | **0** | **0** | **0** |

Four further checks are against published work rather than against this study's
own code:

| check | scope | result |
|---|---|---|
| **`tape.json` §T1, cell for cell** | every coin × window × venue × stop rule, **both tapes**, seeded and chosen, return / drawdown / trades | **3,456 cells, 0 differ** |
| **reference §3.17's §S2 row table** | the three rows this study prices, worst-window ret/DD, shipped stops, Coinbase tape | **worst \|Δ\| = 0** |
| **window D across tapes** | D sits entirely inside the Kraken extension on both arms, so it must be bit-identical | **84 cells per stop rule, 0 differ** |
| **A2's `equal` arm vs A1's baseline** | same money, same members, two different code paths | **16 checks, 0 differ** |

The first is the load-bearing one: it means this harness **is** §3.16 rather
than an approximation of it, so every difference reported below is the choice
being tested and nothing else. The second means it also reproduces §3.17 by a
third route.

`tape.json`'s own SHA-256 is recorded in the output beside the comparison.

## 2. The third tape — Revolut X's own UK book

**Two claims in the reference are wrong and are corrected by measurement.**
§2.3 says Revolut X serves three years of hourly history and §3.16 says the
venue's own tape "needs a signed endpoint and is unreachable from a harness".
Both are wrong. `GET /1.0/public/candles/{SYM}?interval=240&region=UK` is
**public and keyless**, and it serves **2,257 four-hour bars, 2025-09-11 →
2026-09-22 — one year, not three.** `backtest.ts`'s own header, which says one
year, was right all along. `region=UK` is §4.14's requirement: without it the
endpoint serves the EEA book this account cannot trade.

One year reaches **window A and nothing before it**. So the two-tape backbone of
§3.16 is unchanged across all four windows, and the venue's own book is added as
a third tape **on window A alone**, wherever a verdict in the bear year is
load-bearing: A1, A2 and A4. It is not applied to A3's mechanics and it is not a
fourth dimension anywhere.

**How the shortfall was handled, and counted.** Window A's out-of-sample opens a
day or so before the venue's tape starts, and the rule needs ~100 bars of
warm-up for its slow average before it decides at all — starting the array at
the window's first bar would silence the rule for its first 17 days, which is a
larger distortion than a splice. So Kraken's 4-hour tape is spliced strictly
**before** the venue's first bar, exactly as §3.15 built windows C and D, and
everything the splice supplies is counted:

| what | measured |
|---|---|
| coins with a Revolut X tape | 26 of 27 accepted coins |
| coins priced on it | **26** |
| coins dropped | **1** — HYPE, which has no scored window A on any tape (0.62 y of Coinbase history) |
| coverage floor, written down before any number | **95 %** of window A's scored bars must be the venue's own |
| coverage achieved | min **99.73 %**, median **100 %** |
| scored bars supplied by Kraken | **18 in total**, at most **6** on any one coin — one day at the very start |
| gaps in the venue's own tape over window A | **5 bars**: TON 4, ETC 1. Left in, as §3.16 left Kraken's |
| parameters chosen on this tape | **none** — window A's in-sample is the two years before the venue's book begins. Only the seeded point, which the loop runs and which was fixed long before this window, is priced here |

**How far the venue's prints sit from the two series every table is built on**,
over window A, bar for bar:

| | median of the per-coin medians | worst coin's median | worst coin's p95 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revolut X vs Coinbase | **3.08 bps** | 10.97 | 36.80 |
| Revolut X vs Kraken | **4.73 bps** | 10.06 | 46.49 |

That is the same order as §3.15's Coinbase-vs-Kraken overlap (1.83–11.30 bps
median) — the three tapes are three prints of one market, not three markets.

**And what that is worth in returns.** §3.16's T1, on the one window that has
three tapes — the same seeded rule at Revolut X's costs, 50 cells (25 coins ×
two stop rules):

| | measured |
|---|---|
| widest gap between the three tapes' returns, per cell | median **9.21 points**, p95 **36.68**, max **40.63** |
| cells where the three tapes disagree about the SIGN of the return | **9 of 50** |
| cells where the three tapes disagree about §4.15's verdict | **4 of 50 (8 %)** |
| widest single cell | DOT under the shipped stops: **+10.3 % on Coinbase, +18.5 % on Kraken, −22.2 % on Revolut X's own book** |

That is the same order as §3.16's two-tape T1 (median 4.03, p95 31.3, max 70.4
points; 5.9 % verdict flips) and it says the same thing more loudly: **a
per-coin figure is not a stable object.**

**One thing §3.16 found does NOT extend to the third tape.** §3.16's headline
was that the disagreement between Coinbase and Kraken has *no direction*. The
venue's own tape does. One observation per coin — the median of that coin's two
cells, so the near-duplicate cells cannot vote twice, which is the
pseudo-replication trap §3.16 caught itself in:

| comparison | coins | higher on the venue's tape | median Δ | exact two-sided sign test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revolut X vs **Kraken** | 25 | **4** | **−4.5 pts** | **p = 0.00091** |
| Revolut X vs Coinbase | 25 | 8 | −1.5 pts | p = 0.108 |

Across the 25-coin universe the same rule makes **less** money on the venue's
own prints than on the tape the loop decides from. That is a caution worth
writing down: a per-coin Kraken-tape figure is, on this one window, mildly
optimistic about what the venue's book would have produced.

**It does not carry to the live five, and that is the point of a sleeve.** For
the five coins the row actually runs, the mean gap against Coinbase is **+1.6
points** and against Kraken **−2.5**, and the individual coins go in opposite
directions — **SUI +25.5 points** against Coinbase, **AVAX −15.1** — which is
the same cancellation §3.16 measured. The sleeve is steadier than any of its
members *again*, on a third tape, for the third time.

**The live sleeve in the bear year, on all three tapes.** `trend-4h` · Revolut X
costs · five $20 slots · seeded:

| stop rule | Coinbase (published) | Kraken (the loop's signal) | **Revolut X (the book the orders meet)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| shipped | +8.03 %, DD 11.28 %, **0.71** | +9.16 %, DD 10.92 %, **0.84** | **+10.39 %, DD 10.57 %, 0.98** |
| trail | −0.33 %, DD 11.75 %, −0.03 | +6.54 %, DD 11.64 %, 0.56 | +0.86 %, DD 10.86 %, 0.08 |

Under the stop rule the loop actually runs, the bear year on the venue's own
book is the **best** of the three, not the worst. That is one observation and it
is not a reason to move anything, and it is *not* a model of the live loop —
which decides on Kraken and fills on Revolut X, a split `backtest_fill.ts` owns
and this study never simulates. What it does say is that the row's bear-year case
does not weaken when the price series becomes the venue's own.

## 3. A1 — the coins: no change

**What was asked.** §3.11 point 5's leave-one-coin-out re-run on four windows
and both tapes, and the ADD direction: every coin in the priced universe with
the history, added as a sixth slot. A coin counts as a change only if it
improves the sleeve on the **worst** window under both tapes and both stop
rules.

**Method.** Each arm is the coin SET it proposes, priced as the live row —
seeded parameters, Revolut X costs, equal slots of capital ÷ |coins|. An
equal-weight sleeve's return and drawdown do not depend on the slot size, so a
4-coin arm, the 5-coin incumbent and a 6-coin arm are compared per unit of
capital at risk rather than per dollar deployed. A window's sleeve is the arm's
members that have that window.

**The baseline's worst window is D in every condition** — −0.50 ret/DD under the
shipped stops, −0.29 under the trail. The sideways year is what a change has to
beat.

**25 arms. 3 pass. Chance gives 1.56 to 12.5.**

| arm | Δ worst window (4 conditions) | window A, 3 tapes | §4.15 bar | UK book |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **drop·AVAX** | +0.57 / +0.57 / +0.01 / +0.59 | 1 of 6 | clears **A, B, C** under the shipped stops, **A, C** under the trail — so not both walk-forward windows in every condition | $1.9 m |
| **add·ICP** | +0.17 / +0.17 / +0.25 / +0.25 | **6 of 6** | clears **A and D** in every condition, fails **B** | $171 k |
| **add·ATOM** | +0.10 / +0.10 / +0.11 / +0.17 | 0 of 6 | clears **no window in any condition** | **$17 k** — a sixth of the floor |
| drop·SOL | +0.06 / +0.06 / −0.04 / +0.05 | 0 of 6 | SOL clears A and C | $3.3 m |
| add·XLM | +0.12 / +0.12 / −0.02 / +0.33 | 0 of 6 | clears B and D, fails A | $176 k |

**Why none of them is a change, in order of how decisive the reason is:**

1. **The control.** 3 of 25 arms pass. Under the optimistic null — the four
   evaluations treated as four independent coin flips — chance gives **1.56**
   and P(≥ 3) = **0.203**. Under the conservative null — the four evaluations
   move together, which is much closer to the truth, and the measured agreement
   rate across them is **0.72** — chance gives **12.5** and 3 is far *fewer*
   than chance. Neither null makes 3 interesting.
2. **The bar admits none of them.** §4.15 as §3.8 tightened it requires the four
   tests on **both** walk-forward windows plus a UK book over $100k a day.
   **ATOM's book is $17k** — it could not be traded at this size on Revolut X
   whatever a backtest says, and it clears zero windows anyway. **ICP** clears
   the book test and clears windows A and D, but **fails window B** in every
   condition, so the bar does not admit it. `addCandidatesAdmittedByTheBar` in
   the JSON is **empty**.
3. **Every passing arm buys window D somewhere else.** drop·AVAX is +0.57 in D
   and **−2.35 in C** and −0.56 in A. add·ICP is +0.17 in D and **−0.67 in B**.
   add·ATOM is +0.10 in D, −0.55 in B and −1.13 in C. This is §3.17's S1 result
   in a different search: what helps the sideways year helps by being out of the
   market.
4. **§3.15 already priced a per-coin record.** A coin that cleared one window was
   *less* likely to clear the next than one that failed it, in all four arms of
   that study and all sixteen of §3.16's. A one-window or two-window pass is not
   partial credit.

**The third tape's contribution here is specific and it is not what a reader
would guess.** Six arms improve window A on all three tapes — drop·BTC,
drop·ETH, add·BNB, add·ICP, add·POL, add·UNI — where the conservative null gives
12.5 of 25 and the measured three-tape agreement rate is 0.64. Only **one** of
those six also passes the four-window test (ICP), and of the other five, BNB and
POL have books of $19k and $11k. The tape that matters most in the bear year
agrees with the other two often enough that nothing new survives it.

**drop·AVAX deserves a sentence of its own**, because it is the arm with the
largest number attached. AVAX is the sleeve's worst member in the sideways year
(§3.15 measured its leave-one-out at +0.57 in D) and its best in the bear and
strong-bull years. Removing it improves D by 0.57 and costs C **2.35**. It is
the same coin §4.15 uses as its worked example of why a member's own record is
not the argument, and this study reproduces that: **the sleeve is steadier than
any of its parts precisely because the parts disagree about which window they
lose in.**

## 4. A2 — the weights: equal slots stands

§3.11's seven arms plus two §3.11 did not have — the windows-cleared weight and
its look-ahead control — on four windows and both tapes, and then window A on
all three. Six of the nine are a search; `equal` is the null and the two
look-ahead arms are bounds, so neither is counted in the control. Every arm splits the same money — the
row's $100, scaled to the members a window has — between the five coins.

| arm | Δ worst-of-four, by condition | window A, 3 tapes |
|---|---|---|
| **equal** (the null) | — | — |
| inverse volatility | **+0.11 / +0.11 / +0.06 / +0.22** | −0.39 / −0.50 / **−0.60** / −0.20 / −0.52 / −0.30 |
| equal risk | 0 / 0 / +0.05 / +0.17 | 0 / 0 / 0 / −0.21 / −0.52 / −0.29 |
| per-entry volatility scaling | **+0.06 / +0.06 / +0.07 / +0.07** | +0.08 / +0.06 / **−0.21** / +0.03 / +0.07 / **−0.27** |
| weighted by the prior window | 0 / 0 / −0.23 / −0.32 | −0.90 / −1.05 / **−1.25** / −0.49 / −1.03 / −0.58 |
| concentrated on the prior window's clearers | 0 / 0 / −0.37 / −0.32 | −0.38 / −0.86 / **−0.96** / −0.63 / −0.44 / −0.20 |
| **windows cleared** (new) | 0 / 0 / 0 / **−0.32** | −0.26 / −0.70 / **−0.77** / −0.24 / −0.48 / −0.29 |
| *weighted by the scored window's own return* (look-ahead) | *+2.41 / +2.41 / +1.68 / +2.59* | *6 of 6* |
| *windows cleared over all four windows* (look-ahead) | *−0.14 / −0.14 / 0 / 0* | *3 of 6* |

**Two arms beat equal slots on the worst-of-four ranking. Neither beats it in
the bear year on a single one of the three tapes — and the venue's own book is
where they look worst.** Inverse volatility is −0.60 in window A on the venue's
own tape and per-entry scaling is −0.21 there while it is +0.08 on Coinbase.
That is exactly the case the third tape was added to catch: a per-arm comparison
that survives two tapes and dies on the one that is the book.

The control says the same, more weakly: 2 of 6 arms, against **0.375** expected
under the optimistic null (P = 0.0495, borderline) and **3.0** under the
conservative one (P = 0.891). The window-A three-tape test settles it: **0 of
6.**

**Equal risk is not a separate idea, measured again.** Under the shipped stop
rule its delta is **exactly 0** in every condition, because the binding stop
distance at every entry is the 8 % floor, so 1/distance is the same number for
every coin. §3.11 said this on two windows; it is a fact about the stop, not a
result, and it holds wherever the floor binds.

**The new arm fails, as §3.15 predicted, and fails twice.** Weighting by how
many earlier windows a coin clears is 0 of 4 honestly (−0.32 in the condition
where it moves at all) and **0 of 4 with look-ahead** — counting all four
windows including the one being scored does not rescue it either (−0.14, −0.14,
0, 0). So the failure is not "the past is a noisy estimate of the future"; it is
that the windows-cleared count carries no information about the sleeve even when
it is computed with perfect hindsight.

**And the lever is not the problem.** The return look-ahead bound — weighting by
the coin's actual return in the window being scored — is **+1.68 to +2.59**, and
6 of 6 in window A. There is a great deal of money in knowing which coin will
win. Every honest way of guessing it, including the one that is most obviously
worth trying, is worth zero or less.

## 5. A3 — the mechanics: the ranking moves, the settings do not

§3.13 settled the cooldown, all-in/all-out and the stop surface on **two**
windows. Window D was not available. So the question is not whether anything
fixes D — §3.17 answered that, 0 of 33 gate arms — but whether these three
settings look different once D is in the ranking. Each row carries its rank by
the worst of **four** windows and by the worst of **A and B alone**, the ranking
§3.13 used.

**The headline is the rank correlation between the two rankings:**

| search | settings | rank correlation (Spearman) between the four-window and two-window rankings, per condition |
|---|---|---|
| cooldown 0–8 | 9 | **+0.40 / +0.40 / −0.75 / −0.75** |
| all-in/all-out vs tranches | 12 | **+0.39 / −0.52 / −0.08 / −0.22** |
| stop surface (floor × time stop) | 24 | **−0.10 / −0.54 / −0.81 / −0.78** |

Adding one window **re-orders these searches from end to end**, and under the
`trail` stop rule it comes close to reversing them. Under the shipped stops the best cooldown on the
worst-of-two is **0** and on the worst-of-four it is **8**; under the trail it is
the other way round (7 and 0). The best stop surface on the worst-of-two is
`floor=10%|time=none` and on the worst-of-four `floor=15%|time=40`. **Nothing is
good on both rankings in any condition.**

**And yet no setting changes**, because of one number:

> **42 arms. 9 beat the shipped setting on the worst of four windows in all four
> conditions. 0 of 42 beat it on EVERY window.**

Every winner is buying the sideways year with one of the other three.

**The cooldown stays at 2.** The whole 0–8 grid returns **−0.50** in window D
except `reentryBars=8` at −0.46, and is **identical** in windows A and B (0.71)
across the entire grid. The grid spans **0.04** of return over drawdown. 0 of 8
arms pass. The ranking reverses across a plateau that is flat to two decimal
places — which is what re-ranking noise looks like, and is §3.13's own finding
reached from a fourth window.

**All-in / all-out stays.** 1 of 11 arms passes — "in 1, out half at 3×ATR" —
against 0.69 expected under the optimistic null (P = 0.508) and 5.5 under the
conservative one. It ranks **3rd of 12 on the worst-of-four and 11th of 12 on
the worst-of-two**: it wins D and loses A and B, which is the signature of an
arm fitted to one window. Pyramiding — §3.13's near-miss, which won both its
windows by +0.9 — does not pass here in any form.

**The stop surface stays at the 8 % floor with no time stop.** 8 of 23 arms pass
on the worst-of-four, and that number needs reading carefully:

- All 8 are **one idea**: a time stop at 20 bars (3.3 days), plus two at 40 bars
  with tight floors. §3.13 said the same of its own 215-arm stop grid, and it is
  the right caution here too.
- Under the conservative null, 8 of 23 against **11.5 expected** is fewer than
  chance (P = 0.953). Under the optimistic null it looks significant
  (P = 4.9 × 10⁻⁵), and the optimistic null is the wrong one: the four
  evaluations are one calendar scored twice on two tapes that agree to a few
  bps, and the measured agreement rate across them is **0.65**.
- Every one of the 8 gives back windows A and B. `floor=8%|time=20` takes D from
  −0.50 to −0.32 and takes the worst of A and B from **0.71 to 0.40**.
- The sideways trade-off is measured, not asserted: across the 23 settings,
  Pearson(Δ window D, Δ worst of A and B) is **−0.01 / −0.38 / −0.86 / −0.93**
  by condition. Under the stop rule §3.13 was computed on, helping D costs A and
  B almost perfectly one-for-one.
- **The shipped floor was never the best setting and is not claimed to be**: it
  ranks 17th of 24 on the worst-of-four and 18th on the worst-of-two under the
  shipped stops. What the evidence supports is that nothing beats it on both
  rankings, and nothing beats it on every window.

**The honest summary of A3**: window D changes the *order* of these searches
completely and changes the *answer* in none of them. A setting chosen on the new
ranking would be a setting chosen on one window, which is what the four-window
method exists to prevent.

## 6. A4 — the venue: settled, and the question should stop being asked

Asked one way only, as instructed: is there **any** coin, on four windows and
both tapes, whose Kraken figure beats its Revolut X figure on the **worst**
window? Same rule, same tape, same calendar, same seeded parameters; only the
cost schedule changes.

| measurement | result |
|---|---|
| coin × window × condition cells | **372** |
| cells where Kraken's return is higher | **3** (Revolut X higher in 369) — sign test p = **1.8 × 10⁻¹⁰⁵** |
| coins where Kraken beats Revolut X on the **worst** window, in any condition | **1** — SUI/USD, in **1** of 4 conditions |
| …of those, under the stop rule `tick.ts` actually runs | **0** |
| window A on the venue's own book: cells | **50**, Revolut X higher in **all 50** |
| worst Revolut X round trip, any priced coin | **53.8 bps** (TON) |
| best Kraken round trip, any priced coin | **80.0 bps** (BTC) |

The single exception is SUI under `trail·coinbase`, where SUI's worst window is A
and Revolut X reads −0.41 against Kraken's +0.03. `trail` is the intra-bar ATR
stop **the loop stopped running on 2026-09-21** (§3.13), and the mechanism is a
stop-path accident: Kraken's higher fee raises the position's average cost,
which moves the 8 % floor, which changes whether a stop fires. Under the shipped
stops no coin beats on the worst window in any condition.

**The reason this will not change is structural, not statistical.** The worst
round trip on Revolut X across all 25 priced coins is cheaper than the best one
on Kraken. Since the rule and the tape are identical in both columns and only
the fee schedule differs, the Kraken column can only match the Revolut X one
through a stop-path accident — which is precisely what the one exception is. It
has now been asked four times (§3.11 point 3, §3.12, §3.14, here) and answered
no four times, on 952 cells and then on 372 more plus 50 on the venue's own
book. **The answer changes when a fee tier moves, not when a backtest is
re-run.** It should not be re-asked without one.

## 8. Multiple comparisons, all of it

Every search in this file, added up. An arm passes only by beating the incumbent
on the **worst** of its four windows in **all four** evaluations (two tapes ×
two stop rules).

| search | arms | passed | expected, independent null | expected, fully-correlated null |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 — the coins | 25 | 3 | 1.56 (P = 0.203) | 12.5 (P = 1) |
| A2 — the weights | 6 | 2 | 0.38 (P = 0.050) | 3.0 (P = 0.891) |
| A3 — cooldown | 8 | 0 | 0.50 | 4.0 |
| A3 — sizing | 11 | 1 | 0.69 (P = 0.508) | 5.5 (P = 1) |
| A3 — stop surface | 23 | 8 | 1.44 (P = 4.9 × 10⁻⁵) | 11.5 (P = 0.953) |
| A4 — the venue | 25 | 1 | 1.56 (P = 0.801) | 12.5 (P = 1) |
| **whole study** | **98** | **15** | **6.13 (P = 0.0011)** | **49 (P = 1)** |

Plus **22,788** grid points evaluated to choose parameters and measure plateaus.

**The two nulls, stated exactly, and which one to read.** An arm must win four
near-duplicate evaluations. If those four were independent fair coins the
expected pass count is arms × ¹⁄₁₆; if they move together it is arms × ½. Both
expectations are exact however correlated the *arms* are (linearity of
expectation); the tail probabilities additionally assume the arms are
independent, which variants of one row are not, so every tail is a floor and not
a p-value.

**The truth is much nearer the correlated end, and that is measured rather than
asserted.** The share of arms on which all four evaluations agree is **0.72** in
A1, **0.83** in A2 and **0.63–0.65** in A3 — and the two tapes agree on prices to
a few basis points, so the four evaluations are one calendar scored four nearly
identical ways. Read the right-hand column: **15 passes of 98 arms where chance
gives 49** is not a study that found something.

**The caution the arm count deserves.** The 24-point stop surface is one idea in
24 forms and the 12-point sizing grid is one idea in 12; counting them as 34
independent arms overstates the search, exactly as §3.13's report said of its
own 262. The two searches whose arms genuinely differ are **the coin set** and
**the weighting** — and those are the two that land most clearly inside chance,
and the two the third tape independently refuses.

## 9. What this could not settle, all of it

1. **The third tape is one year and one window.** Everything said about the
   venue's own book applies to the bear year only. There is no Revolut X print
   for windows B, C or D and there never will be — the venue's history begins in
   2025-09. In particular, the finding that the venue's tape reads **lower per
   coin than Kraken's** (21 of 25 coins, p = 0.00091) is a single-window
   observation and is not evidence about any other regime.
2. **No parameter is chosen on the venue's tape.** Window A's in-sample is the
   two years before that book exists, so the third tape carries the seeded point
   only. A "chosen parameters" column for it cannot exist.
3. **The splice at the start of window A is Kraken's**, 18 scored bars across 26
   coins, plus ~100 bars of warm-up per coin. That is small and counted, but it
   is not zero, and the warm-up means the venue's tape does not set the state the
   rule enters window A with.
4. **This study never splits signal from fill.** Every arm here prices signal
   AND fill on one series at a time, including the third-tape arm, which decides
   and fills on Revolut X's own prints. The live loop does neither: it decides on
   Kraken's candles (`signal_venue`) and fills on Revolut X's book. That split is
   **not** this study's subject — `backtest_fill.ts` / `fill.json` landed on
   `main` in parallel with this run and owns it. Read the two together: this one
   says the live row's four choices do not move when the tape moves; that one
   says what the cross-venue fill costs.
5. **Window D is the binding window for every A1 and A3 arm**, so this study's
   verdicts are more sensitive to the sideways year than to anything else. D has
   21 coins nested inside C's in-sample and is a single draw.
6. **SUI has two windows, not four**, so the sleeve in C and D is four coins at
   $80 rather than five at $100, and SUI is absent from exactly the window that
   decides A1.
7. **A3 was not given the third tape**, by instruction and because its verdict is
   set by window D, which the venue's tape cannot see at all.
8. **The rows were not re-asked**, only the live row's definition. `trend-1h` is
   priced on neither new tape.
9. **`agent_risk`'s daily loss limit was not simulated**, here as in every study
   before it.
10. **Nothing here measures execution.** Fills are the next bar's open at the
    touch; the maker probe (`0042`) is the measurement that settles what a
    resting order does on the real book, and it is collecting.
11. **"Not significant" means "not detectable at this size"** — 25 coin arms, 6
    weighting arms, 4 evaluations. It does not mean "absent".

deno run --allow-read --allow-write \
  supabase/functions/agents/backtest_set2.ts \
