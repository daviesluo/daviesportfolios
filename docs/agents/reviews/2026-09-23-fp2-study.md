## The answer

**Nothing venue-unique is worth money, and nothing passed a pre-registered test.** I derived 20 ideas,
19 of them not among the first programme's 31 and one of its kills re-opened on the newly public trade
history. 15 die on arithmetic or a measurement, one needs a signed call, one is out of scope, and one is a
protective behaviour rather than a strategy. The three that survived went to pre-registered tests, and
all three lose out of sample:

* **T1 — 0 % quotes inside Revolut X's resident market maker's spread on its thin long tail** (the only
  books where the spread is wide enough for a once-a-minute quote): **−$69.97 over 144 book-days** (null
  p95 +$26.61, stress −$68.93); its fills come just before the coin falls, and most prints there are a
  $0.10 bot.
* **T2 — 0 % quotes 50 bps from Binance waiting for UK-only sweeps** (the first programme's ideas 3–4, now on
  trades): **−$99.81 over 357 round trips** (null p95 +$72.15, stress −$262.64); every book loses.
* **T3 — bids under Binance's liquid-staking tokens (WBETH, BNSOL)**: **−$11.38 over 2,186 round trips** on
  440 untouched days (stress −$452.33); a resting bid cannot tell the token's own wick from its base's move.

**Why, in one number: h/σ₁ₘ.** Revolut X's resident maker quotes wide — a median 27 bps half-spread across
183 UK USD books against Binance's 0.5–10 — but on every busy book that spread is smaller than one minute
of price movement (h/σ₁ₘ 0.14–0.98). A loop that looks once a minute and may place 1,000 orders a day
cannot keep a quote inside it; it needs h ≥ 2.7 σ₁ₘ for one book. Only 42 thin books clear that ($124k a
day between them), and there the inside quote gets the fills that come before a fall (T1).

**Three facts worth keeping, none of them an edge:**
1. **In a crash Revolut X's UK book shelters a seller; it does not wick.** On 2025-10-10 Binance's alts
   fell 60–85 % while on five of six books the UK lows stayed 34–192 % higher (BCH: 0.8 % lower), and UK
   bids sat 1–85 % above Binance in 11–57 minutes on those five (runs of up to 8 minutes). A UK crash ladder would have bought nothing; the live
   row's protective sells there would have been paid above Binance.
2. **Binance's 24/7 tokens of closed markets are priced efficiently now.** About 60 US stocks
   (NVDAB, TSLAB, MSTRB, CRCLB…) price the next open with a slope of 0.98 (corr 0.99, 315 nights); EUR's
   weekend fade leaves 5–12 bps; PAXG's weekend overshoot paid +33–44 bps (mean, nearly all on pops, before
   costs) in 2024–25 and has shrunk to +10–13 since March 2026 (XAUT +4–8), below a 20 bps round trip.
3. **A bot prints $0.10 trades at the UK touch** (66–95 % of the long-tail books' prints). Fills read from
   the tape — PR3's forward test included — need a size floor or a volume cap.

What differs between the two venues is still cost and reach, not a rule; the combination this evidence
supports is Binance as the fair value for Revolut X's books, and Revolut X as the UK book that pays a
seller well in a panic.

## 1. Where return can come from, derived from what the account has

The first programme started from a list of return sources. This one starts from the account: what it
owns that other traders don't, and what it can't do. A return has to come from something scarce.

**What the account has**

| endowment | what it is worth |
|---|---|
| Free resting orders on Revolut X (0 % maker) | a free option written to the market: it costs nothing until someone trades with it |
| A UK identity | access to Revolut X's UK books, its GBP books and GBP rails; nothing else in the world trades on those books except UK customers |
| A Binance account | the deepest price in crypto (a fair value for every thin book), 24/7 books of assets whose own markets close (gold, stocks, EUR), wrapped and staked forms |
| Size of $100s–$1,000s | it can live inside books too small for anyone professional |
| Patience | it can hold for minutes to days |

**What it cannot do**

| constraint | consequence |
|---|---|
| Spot only (UK retail: no margin, no derivatives) | it cannot short; it can sell only what it holds |
| 1,000 order placements a day on Revolut X | about 0.7 a minute for everything it does; a quote kept in place against a moving price costs placements |
| One look a minute | anything that needs a reaction inside a minute must be done by an order already resting |
| 9 bps (Revolut X) / 10 bps (Binance) to take | a taker idea needs more than 20 bps of edge a round trip |

**Who can pay it**, and the one condition each needs:

1. **Impatient takers** pay the spread → liquidity provision. Needs: the spread earned > adverse selection
   + inventory cost, *and a quote fresh enough to be at the front* — which for this account means the
   refresh arithmetic below.
2. **Holders of stale prices** → relative value, in three directions: **across venues** (a UK book that
   lags Binance), **across forms** (a wrapped or staked token against its base), and **across time** (a
   24/7 token of an asset whose own market is shut). Needs: a gap larger than the cost of trading it,
   often enough, and a way to be on the right side without shorting.
3. **Forced sellers** (liquidations, capitulation) → tail liquidity. Needs: depth of the wick > the
   continuation after it, and more than one event.
4. **Scheduled or structural flows** → needs a schedule that survives the cost of front-running it.
5. **The venue** (rebates, promotions, airdrops) → needs a signed call to see.
6. **Risk premia** (beta, carry) → not venue-specific; the benchmark, not an answer.

**The inequality that decides liquidity provision for a one-minute loop.** A resting quote that must stay
within ±w of a fair value moving σ₁ₘ per minute needs a new placement every ≈ (w/σ₁ₘ)² minutes (first
passage of a random walk). With ~800 placements a day left for one book and one side at a time, w ≥ 1.34
σ₁ₘ. To stay inside a half-spread h with room on both sides needs h ≥ 2w, so **h ≥ 2.7 σ₁ₘ**. Quoting two
books halves the budget per book (h ≥ 3.8 σ₁ₘ); six books need h ≥ 6.6 σ₁ₘ. This single number, h/σ₁ₘ,
decides every market-making idea below. The first programme priced the majors' touch (h ≈ 1 bp); nobody had
measured h/σ₁ₘ across the venue.

## 5. What a combination of the two venues looks like

Nothing to put money on. What the evidence says the two venues do together:

1. **Binance is the fair value for every Revolut X book.** One public request (`/api/v3/ticker/bookTicker`)
   prices all 183 UK USD books with a Binance twin; the first programme's point about Revolut X's own
   `index_price` stands beside it. It is what T1 and T2 quoted around.
2. **Revolut X's UK book shelters a seller in a crash.** On 2025-10-10 its resident maker's bids stayed above
   Binance's by 1–85 % in 11–57 minutes on five of six books while Binance's alts wicked 60–85 %. The live row's protective
   sells on Revolut X would have met those bids. That is a property to know, not an edge to farm: it needs
   the coin already held there, and it happened once in the year.
3. **Binance's 24/7 books of closed markets** (gold, EUR, ~60 US stocks) are priced efficiently now: their
   closed-hours moves predict the reopen with slopes of 0.98 (stocks) and 1.02 (gold since March 2026).
4. **Market making across the two** (Binance's price, Revolut X's free resting orders) is possible only
   where h ≥ 3σ₁ₘ, i.e. on the thin long tail, and there it lost (T1).

## 6. Where this differs from the first programme, and why

1. **The first programme priced quoting on Revolut X's majors** (h ≈ 1 bp) and killed it by adverse
   selection and the order budget. This one measured the whole UK book set: the resident maker quotes a
   median 27 bps half-spread across 183 USD books, which reopened the question — and closed it with a
   different number, h/σ₁ₘ, derived from the 1,000-order budget and the one-minute clock. Below ~2.7 no
   budget-limited quote can stay inside the spread; every busy book is below 1.
2. **Its ideas 3–4 (deep quotes for UK-only sweeps) were killed on quote-built candles as "cents a day".**
   Re-opened on prints (T2), the verdict stands on trades and with a reason: sweeps ≥ 30 bps beyond Binance
   are 0.2–1.7 a day a book, and a deep quote's fills come with Binance's own moves (median 60-minute markout
   of its 566 in-sample fills of $10 or more −17 bps, $-weighted −101) and with the crash.
3. **Its idea 30 (a de-peg lottery) was untestable; the coin version is now measured**: in the one crash on
   record the UK book did not wick below Binance, it lagged above it. A UK crash ladder catches nothing.
4. **It killed liquid-staking tokens on accrual (1–6 bps over a hold).** Their wicks are a different
   mechanism, measured and pre-registered here (T3): they fail because a resting bid fills on the base's
   moves too.
5. **It did not look along the time axis.** Tokens of markets that close (gold at weekends, EUR at weekends,
   stocks overnight) are the one place a 24/7 venue can hold a stale price by construction. Gold's weekend
   overshoot existed in 2024–25 (+33–44 bps, nearly all on pops) and has since shrunk below a Binance round trip, like
   PR3's gap; stocks and EUR leave 5–12 bps.
6. **A new data fact for anyone reading the tape** (PR3's paper test included): a bot prints $0.10 trades at
   the UK touch on many books on some days (every pulled book on 2026-01-23; 66–95 % of the prints on the
   long-tail books). A print through a quote proves a price, not capacity; fills read from the tape need a
   size floor or a volume cap.

## 8. Checks, and what could still mislead

* **Freezes before results.** T1/T2 frozen 14:20:04Z (`e8294ebe…`); the first strategy number of any kind
  (an in-sample-only debugging run) came after it, and the out-of-sample run at 14:51 after the pull ended.
  T3 frozen 14:30:14Z (`89f3f450…`), run at 14:31. Before T1/T2's freeze only in-sample prints were read
  (plus pull-log counts); nothing of WBETH before 2024-10-01 was read before T3's.
* **Determinism.** Two runs each: T1/T2 `98630d42…` twice, T3 `14dae6ed…` twice.
* **One trip checked by hand** (W-USD, 2025-11-12): bid set at the 00:12:00 turn at the previous close's
  fair × (1 − d) = 0.061208, live 00:12:30, filled 00:12:33 by a sell print at 0.0609 (strictly below, the
  print's full 2,902.53 W); ask re-set at 00:15 and lifted at 00:15:43 by a buy print at 0.0616; +$0.4434.
* **Rate limit.** Every Revolut X request went through the shared file lock (≥ 1.1 s between request starts
  from any process); 1,822 tape requests; every one of the 1,125 book-day pulls ended complete with HTTP 200
  (the helper waits out a 429's Retry-After and retries; retries are not logged, so their count is unknown).
* **What could flatter or hurt the tests.** The UK queue is not modelled: a quote better than every resting
  price is at the front, so a print through it fills it — that flatters nothing a real order would not get,
  but arbitrageurs who would pick off a stale quote of ours are absent from history (the price is the same,
  the timing sooner). Taker exits assume $200 of depth at the UK bid. Sampled days are simulated alone
  (flat at midnight). T1's books were chosen with today's spreads. The dust bot inflates T1's trip count
  (disclosed above) but not its P&L (the $10+ trips carry −$70.35 of −$69.97). The gold holdout could not
  be built (hourly gold before 2024-05 is not reachable keylessly from here); gold's verdict rests on the
  period split, not a pre-registered test.
* **My own text had one inconsistency**: bar 6 says ≤ 800 placements a day while the rule's token bucket
  (800 a day plus 40 at the start) allows up to ~840; both readings are reported, and neither changes a
  verdict.
