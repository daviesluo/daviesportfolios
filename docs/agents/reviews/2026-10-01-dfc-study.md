## What it says

- **The pattern shrank to a third and lost its significance.** Before 2016 the spread between the buy-back and selling
  windows was 15.46 bp a day (t 3.82, 1990–2015); over the ten held-out years it is 4.87 on the settlement-adjusted
  windows and 7.97 on the paper's, and on the paper's windows the second half (2021-04 → 2026-07) is negative.
- **The one rule that could pay lost money.** Out of the index over the selling window and in otherwise, it gave up
  2.87 % a year (H1) and 1.21 % (H2) net of 8 bps, and 2.27 % / 0.61 % even at 3 bps (descriptive): the market's excess
  return was 13.54 % a year (volatility 18.36 %), and every day out of it cost more than the dip it avoided.
- **The other month-end rules are worse** (descriptive, against buy and hold at 8 bps): PB-only −8.65 % (H1) and −7.45 %
  (H2) a year, TOM-only −11.31 %, at about 7–9 % volatility against 18 %.
- **What it cannot say**, as the pre-registration warned: a fail rules out an effect of the screen's size, not a
  smaller one; it does not say why the effect faded (decay after publication is one reading, the shorter settlement
  cycles another).

## What follows

- US-equity calendar rules are closed at this account's size, with EQ1's and EQ2's other closures (research round §4
  and §8). Nothing goes to paper; no Trading 212 trading key is needed.
- TAC (the Treasury auction cycle on IDTL) and IS49 (industry seasonality) stay drafted and unfrozen; neither is
  recommended on its power alone (about 0.42 and 0.32–0.68).
