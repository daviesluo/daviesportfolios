## Where the fair value is published

The fair-value series is Deribit `public/get_tradingview_chart_data`
for `BTC-PERPETUAL` with `resolution=1D`, from `www.deribit.com`. The public
page is `https://docs.deribit.com/#public-get_tradingview_chart_data`.
`ticks` are the candle open times in milliseconds, and `close` is the last
trade of that candle. Every tick in the pull used for the count is 08:00 UTC
and the next tick is 86,400,000 milliseconds later. The close is published
when the candle ends, at `tick + 86400000`, which is the next 08:00 UTC.
A decision at a Binance midnight entry uses a Deribit close only when that
end is strictly earlier. The candle that ends at 08:00 UTC on the entry's
own calendar day is still in the future at midnight, so it is not used. The
newest usable close was published at 08:00 UTC on the previous calendar day.

The price being judged is the finished close of the USDT book. That close is
not the fair value. The open of that book is the fill and is not an input.
Binance spot, the coin-margined book and the USDT book are not read as this
fair value.

## The idea

**DBWDN.** Fair value is the Deribit BTC-PERPETUAL daily close. The basis is the finished USDT close over that fair value. It finished positive and strictly above the basis six days earlier, so the next USDT open sells a premium to Deribit that has widened. Sell the USDT book for ten days. The other trade sells the USDT book for ten days on these same prices when that wider premium is absent. Same side. The edge is turned off. Not the opposite side. Not an unconditional hold. The entry open is not an input. One leg.

The widening set is the shorter one at a strict premium: 133 against 223. No extra discount was required.
