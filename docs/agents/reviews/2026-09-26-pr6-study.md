# PR6: 0 % quotes at par on Revolut X's USD stablecoin books fail — the stops eat the spread (2026-09-26)

The fp5 review ranked this second of what is worth running. It is PR5's rule with the fair value pinned at 1.0000:
0 % bids and asks 1, 2 and 3 ticks either side of par on Revolut X's UK USDC-USD and USDT-USD books, scored on the
venue's keyless public prints with PR5's frozen simulator. The pre-registration
(`2026-09-26-pr6-revx-usd-par-prereg.md`, sha256 45e69d0d…a54) was frozen on main by 714a71c0, before any fill,
order, position or P&L was computed. This file reports what the frozen run found. The scripts, inputs and result are in
`docs/agents/backtests/pr6/`.

## Answer: it fails two of the seven conditions. It makes money, and not enough to be worth running.

Primary window: USDC-USD 2025-11-27 → 2026-09-26 (303 days), USDT-USD 2025-12-17 → 2026-09-26 (283 days).

## What was checked before any of this was believed

* **Every hash matched** before anything was scored. That covers:
  * the pre-registration;
  * PR5's simulator (56fbad85…) and its checks (7dedbaa1…);
  * the eight scripts and two outputs that ran before the freeze;
  * both print files. They are committed gzipped, and their decompressed bytes hash to the frozen 91a1287c… and
    0568e84c…. No pull was repeated.
* **The checks on made-up prints passed before a PR6 print was opened.** PR5's own checks (`test_sim_logic.py`) exited 0
  with "all synthetic checks pass". This layer's 13 checks (`checks.py`) passed too. Each check bites when the layer is
  broken on purpose:
  * with PR5's own rungs, 7 checks fail;
  * with the guard at 31 ticks instead of 30, both edge checks fail;
  * with the two books' half spreads swapped, both stop-cost checks fail.
* **Every one of the 8,341 primary trips was checked against the raw prints.** None failed. The checks were:
  * the entry sits at its rung;
  * a print strictly through it carries its fill time, inside its fill minute;
  * its size is exactly the cap;
  * every maker exit is at par, with a print strictly through par in its exit minute, at least two minutes after
    the fill;
  * every taker exit is a 24-hour stop or the end of the data.
* **Two runs of the scorer wrote byte-identical files** (`pr6.json`, sha256 a9cefc21…).
* **The null.** A twin's P&L is PR5's `exit_only()` for $1, times the trip's dollars; with X = 1 that function is linear
  in its size. Three whole null values were computed again with direct calls at each trip's own size. The difference
  was 0.0 in all three.
* **No position was left out.** Neither book printed in the window's last minute, so PR5's end-of-data rule dropped
  none.

## The primary arm

* **Size and rate.** 8,341 round trips on $372,735 of fills. That is +1.0 bp per dollar filled and $0.12 a day.

* **Where the profit went.**
  * 7,911 exits at par as a maker earned +$68.02, 1.93 bps each on average.
  * 427 positions (5.1 %) waited 24 hours without a print through par on their exit side and were stopped: −$30.65,

## Stress and the null

**Stress** made −$8.41 on 6,611 round trips. In this arm the stop costs twice as much, and every fill needs a print one
more tick through. The maker exits earned +$54.49, and 488 stops lost $62.05. Both books lost: USDC-USD −$7.03 and
USDT-USD −$1.38.

**The circular-shift null.** The 282 values ran from −$197.70 to −$56.71, with a mean of −$155.78 and a p95 of
−$130.65. The rule beats every shift.

**PR5's random-time null** is descriptive, not in the bar. Its mean was −$112.70, its p95 −$102.81 and its maximum
−$90.32; no draw reached the rule.

Both nulls are far below zero. A twin buys or sells at a traded price rather than at a limit 1–3 ticks from par, then
waits for par or the stop, as the rule's positions do. So condition 2 shows only that the rule's limit entries beat
entries at a traded price. Whether the rule is worth money was always condition 7's question.

## The last three months, the last four weeks, and the regime

* **Last three months** (2026-06-26 → 09-26, condition 7): 1,599 trips and +$5.56. That is $0.060 a day, or 1.84 %/yr
  on $1,200. Maker exits earned +$14.16; 120 stops cost $8.37; 3 closes at the end of the data cost $0.23.
* **Last four weeks** (2026-08-29 → 09-26, reported apart): 374 trips and +$1.54. That is $0.055 a day, or 1.67 %/yr.
* **The regime.** PR5's measure is the median gap between adjacent buy and sell prints within 60 s.
  * From the week of 2026-08-24 (W35) it read 1.0–3.0 bps on USDC-USD and 1.5–4.0 bps on USDT-USD, every week.
  * Before that, the weekly medians centred on 12.5 bps (USDC-USD, range 3.0–35.0) and 11.0 bps (USDT-USD, range
    2.0–22.5).
  * So the USD books appear to have tightened in the same week as PR5's GBP books. But each week since rests on only
    4–13 pairs: read it as a direction, not a size.

  its minute's volume.

* **No post-only refusal.** Refusing a post-only order the market is already through helps here: without it, there
  are 681 stops. In PR5 it went the other way.
* **None of these arms is a pass.** Each would need a pre-registration of its own.

## The de-peg guard

* **USDT-USD.** It printed more than 30 bps from par 349 times on 11 days. All but two of those prints were below par;
  the lowest was 0.9900. The guard darkened 1,392 minutes in 47 runs on nine of those days: 2026-01-24/25, 01-30/31,
  02-14, and 05-26 → 05-29. On 2026-05-28 alone it darkened 553 minutes.
* **It changed no trip.** With and without it, the trip lists are identical one for one. In those minutes the market
  was through the bids, so post-only would have refused them anyway, and nothing traded up to the asks. Its only
  effect was 212 more orders on USDT-USD (11,013 against 10,801): the withdrawals, and the re-placements after them.
* **USDC-USD.** Its one print beyond 30 bps was a buy at 1.0081, inside a four-print sweep on 2026-08-28 at 14:16:38.
  The same millisecond ended near par, so no turn was dark.

## Orders and mark to market

* **Orders.** 18,424 over 303 days: 60.8 a day on average, and 268 on the busiest day (2026-06-05). No day went over
  700. A fixed fair never re-prices, so orders come only from placements, exits, and re-placements after a refusal.
* **Mark to market.** Every open position was valued at its book's last print at each UTC midnight. The worst drawdown
  of that daily equity was $2.92, reached on 2026-07-02. The worst open value was −$0.92, on 2026-05-29. The window
  ends at +$37.14 with nothing open.

## The whole tape (descriptive)

* **From 2025-09-26:** 14,970 trips and +$87.80.
* **Before each book's primary start:** 6,629 trips and +$50.66 (62 days of USDC-USD, 82 of USDT-USD). That is richer

* **From each book's primary start,** the whole-tape run gives exactly the primary's trips: 8,341 and +$37.14. With a

  the primary start.

## What it means

PR6 asked whether pinning the fair value at par removes the adverse selection that kills quotes on coin books. It
removes the staleness, not the adverse selection. A quote 1–3 ticks from par fills when the book moves away from par.
About one position in twenty then waits a day without a print back through par, and is stopped at about 15 bps.
Those stops take 45 % of the maker profit at the venue's real fee, and all of it under stress. What is left is +1 bp
per dollar filled: 3.9 %/yr over the window and 1.8 %/yr in the last three months, below cash.

The power check's warning held in direction: condition 7 decided the test. The rule traded more than fp5's hourly
closes suggested (27.5 round trips a day, not 3.3). It earned ten times the $0.012 a day that prior implied, and still
less than half of what 8 %/yr needs.

## Deviations and notes

There is no deviation from the frozen rule, data, windows, arms, null or bar. For the record:

1. **One layer check was wrong in its first draft.** Its expected no-guard answer ignored post-only refusal: its
   made-up far print sat below par, so post-only refused the bids with or without the guard. It was corrected before
   any PR6 print was read. The far print now sits above par, so only the guard keeps the bids out. Every check the
   pre-registration lists is there, plus two: each book's stop cost, and the no-guard counterpart of the guard check.
2. **The scorer ran once before its final form.** After that run it gained two descriptive fields: the guard's dark
   runs, and the count of prints beyond 30 bps. It also now opens the print files only after the checks pass; the first
   run hashed their bytes before the checks, without parsing them. Every section the bar reads is identical between that
   run and the final two.
3. **The null's twin P&L** is `exit_only()`'s $1 result times the trip's dollars, as the scorer's docstring says. It
   was checked against direct calls on three whole shifts.
4. **The candles are not committed.** The pre-freeze power check read the venue's UK hourly candles, but only what the
   scorer reads was committed. The candles' hashes are in the pre-registration.
5. **Two pinned one-off scripts carry the path they ran from.** `fp5_candle_volume.py` and `feasibility_synthetic.py`
   name the absolute path of the working copy, and the first also names the fp5 branch it read. They are kept byte for
   byte, because the pre-registration pins their hashes. Neither is needed to reproduce the result.
