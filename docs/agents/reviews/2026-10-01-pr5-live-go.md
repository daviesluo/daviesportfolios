# PR5 live at £120: the strategy, its settings, the paper-test interaction and the go-live checklist (2026-10-01)

Davies, 2026-10-01: PR5's GBP stablecoin quotes go live on their own Revolut X sub-account, topped up to £120, at £10 a
quote; check that every setting is the best supported one and nothing is left worth optimising; raise the daily order
limit from 600 to 900, because `trend-4h-live` never sends 100 orders a day.

Everything below comes from data committed before PR5's paper test began (2026-09-23 15:09 UTC) and from today's public
books. Nothing of PR5's own paper record, its dry-run, PR5V, variant-2 or PR5-W was read. The study is
`backtests/pr5_live/go_live_120.py` → `go_live_120.json`. It imports the frozen `pr5_sim.py` (56fbad85…) and PR5V's
`pr5v_sim.py` (54ad4198…) unchanged, and its port of the frozen minute loop reproduces `simulate()` trip for trip and
order for order on both windows before it reports anything (8,192 trips and $707.90 in the primary window, 102 trips and
$11.6688 after the books tightened).

## Answer first

* **The strategy is PR5 itself**: 0.1 / 0.2 / 0.3 % either side of fair on USDC/GBP and USDT/GBP, twelve rungs of £10.
* **Every setting stays, except two, both changed in code here and pinned:**
  * the order governor: entries withdrawn at **900** POSTs a UTC day (Davies' number), only stops from **950**;
  * a refused exit now waits as the rule's own refused order waits. Before, it went out again every minute.
* **The paper-test interaction is real but small in money.** Resting at the paper's prices, the live orders take 7 of
  PR5's 102 paper trips in the tightened market. The study's P&L moves by +0.5 %, because those are the smallest fills.
  One tick behind would take none, but costs the live test a fifth of its fills. **Recommendation: same price.**
* **Inventory: convert £30 into each coin with the executor's own conversion, after the first live turn.** It costs
  £0.07 at today's books. The account must hold at least £120.20 for the second conversion to pass its GBP check while
  the six bids rest, so **top it up to £121**. Bid-only never supplies the asks: they would stay skipped for good.

## The strategy, and why this one

* **PR5 passed its pre-registered test on nine months of prints it never saw** (reference §3.27): +$707.90 on $1,200,
  ten of ten months positive, far above its random-time null.
* **It is the only rule with a live path.** `agents/quotes_live.ts` carries out the paper engine's decisions order for
  order. It has run in dry-run on this sub-account since 2026-09-24 (migration `0052`, reference §4 item 35).
* **The variants are not candidates.** PR5V and variant-2 have no live path. They need $3,600, three times the capital.
  They are read on 2026-10-28; until then nothing of them may be read beyond their health scalars.
* **At £120, £10 a rung.** On the 28 days after the books tightened (2026-08-26 → 09-23), the frozen rule at £10 a
  rung made **£1.31: £0.047 a day, 14.2 %/yr on £120**, on 102 round trips, 94 % won. By book and side: USDT bids
  £0.570 (34 trips), USDT asks £0.318 (28), USDC bids £0.323 (27), USDC asks £0.097 (13). It is a measurement of real
  post-only fills, not an income.

## The governor

**What the 1,000 is.** Revolut X documents `POST /1.0/orders` as a token bucket: 1,000 tokens, refilled continuously at
1,000 a day (reference §2). It does not say whether the bucket is per key, per sub-account or per user. PR5 has its own
sub-account and key (`_2`); if the bucket is shared, `trend-4h-live` uses at most 40 entries a day (`agent_risk`) and a
handful of exits.

**The tiers.** Entries are withdrawn at 900 (Davies). From 950 nothing but the 24-hour stops goes out. 950 leaves 50
under the 1,000 for the stops, which are always sent, and for the trend row if the budget is shared.

PR5V's simulator at PR5's settings (identical to the frozen rule without a governor), both books on one account, every
placement, re-price, re-placement, exit and stop counted, 2025-12-16 → 2026-09-23 (281 days, mostly the wide market):

| governor | P&L at $100 rungs | days entries withdrawn | POSTs after the entry tier (median / max) | most stops in a day | day bucket's lowest | most POSTs in any 24 h |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| none | $695.91 | — | — | 3 | −448 (breached) | 1,906 |
| 600 / 700 (before) | $580.95 | 73 | 9 / 100 | 3 | 757 | 1,010 |
| **900 / 950** | **$665.68** | **18** | **8 / 50** | **3** | **555** | 1,281 |
| 900 / 930 | $666.85 | 18 | 8 / 30 | 3 | 548 | 1,281 |
| 900 / 970 | $666.40 | 18 | 8 / 70 | 3 | 548 | 1,281 |

* 900 / 950 earns $84.73 more than 600 / 700 over those days at $100 rungs (+14.6 %), and gives up 4.3 % against no
  governor at all, which would have breached the venue's bucket.
* The stops-only tier hardly matters to P&L. 950 was reached on one day of 281; the stops never exceeded 3 a day.
* A UTC-day count lets two busy days meet across midnight (1,281 POSTs inside one 24 hours). The venue's bucket refills
  during them, and its lowest level was 555 of 1,000.
* **In the tightened market nothing binds**: the busiest day sent 441 POSTs; the live executor's own, with the exit fix
  below, at most 431.

Pinned in `quotes_live.test.ts`: the tiers themselves (600 and 700 now "all", 900 "no-entries", 950 "stops-only"), and
a replay where 700 POSTs today still let the six bids go out and 900 withdraws them. Both fail with 600 / 700 restored.

## A refused exit

Entries carry one POST per paper decision. Exits had no such rule: a post-only exit the venue refused (the book was
through its price) went out again on the next turn, every minute, until the venue took it. The paper rule waits instead:
a refused order is re-placed at the first turn whose last print is no longer through it.

The study replays the executor beside the paper rule on the committed prints (the coupled model below):

| | refused exits | exit POSTs | exit fills | live POSTs a day (mean / max) | days over 900 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| tightened market, sent every minute (before) | 872 | 1,150 | 111 | 233 / 545 | 0 |
| tightened market, **waits (now)** | 36 | 310 | 113 | 203 / 431 | 0 |
| wide market, sent every minute (before) | 53,643 | 81,765 | 8,044 | 580 / 2,517 | 63 |
| wide market, **waits (now)** | 1,312 | 29,096 | 8,006 | 387 / 1,801 | 13 |

`exitMayGo` sends a refused exit again only once the paper engine holds a newer print than it held when the exit was
sent, and that print is not through the exit's price (the rule's own `blocks`). Each exit row now records that print as
`request.paperLastPrint`. A 429 (the rate limit, which takes nothing) and a refusal of an earlier holding hold nothing
back. Pinned by a pure test and three replays: the venue refusing at once (400) and after taking the order (read back
`rejected`), and a refusal while the tape shows nothing through the exit (the book moved without a trade). Removing the
wiring fails the three replays; removing any of the three clauses, or the recorded print, fails at least one pin.

## The paper-test interaction

**How.** A live order rests in the venue's book; a paper order does not. A seller sweeping the bids fills every better
price, then the queue already resting at our price, then ours, and only what is left prints beyond it. PR5's paper rule
fills a bid only on a print strictly below it. So when the part of a taker's order that went beyond our price was £10 or
less, our order takes all of it, nothing prints through, and the paper order that print would have filled does not
fill. Our own fill prints AT the price, which proves nothing for the paper. The same holds for asks and for exits.

**First order.** For every fill in PR5's study, the quantity that printed beyond the order's price in the proving
print's taker group (same millisecond, same aggressor), against a £10 order at that price:

* tightened market: **16 of 203 fills (7.9 %)** would have lost their proving print;
* wide market: 1,875 of 16,340 (11.5 %).

**Replayed.** The paper rule and the live book side by side on the committed tape: the executor's £10 orders at the
paper's prices, placed 30 s after the paper counts them live (the dry-run's measured lag), taking flow as above; the
paper rule then reads the tape as it would have printed. With no live order the replay is the frozen rule exactly.

| | paper trips | paper P&L | trips the live took: mean size | live entry fills a day | live P&L a day at £10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| tightened, no live book | 102 | $11.67 | (all trips: $80.03) | | |
| tightened, **same price, 12 rungs** | **95 (−7)** | **+0.49 %** | $21.81 | 3.79 | £0.048 |
| tightened, same price, bids only | 100 (−2) | −0.12 % | $50.39 | 2.18 | £0.030 |
| tightened, one tick behind, 12 rungs | 102 (0) | −0.00 % | — | 3.04 | £0.045 |
| tightened, one tick behind, bids only | 102 (0) | +0.00 % | — | 1.71 | £0.028 |
| wide, no live book | 8,192 | $707.90 | (all trips: $50.46) | | |
| wide, same price, 12 rungs | 7,133 (−1,059) | +0.32 % | $17.75 | 26.9 | £0.426 |
| wide, one tick behind, 12 rungs | 7,747 (−445) | +1.78 % | $13.34 | 23.5 | £0.422 |

By rung in the tightened market (same price, 12 rungs), the trips taken were the 0.1 % rungs': USDC asks 13 → 10, USDC
bids 21 → 19, USDT asks 19 → 17. No 0.2 % or 0.3 % rung lost a trip.

**Why P&L hardly moves.** The paper sizes a fill at the smaller of $100 and 10 % of its minute's printed volume. The
fills a £10 order takes are the ones proven by small takers, in small minutes, so they are small fills: $21.81 on
average against $80.03. The paper order then usually fills on a later, bigger print.

**What it does to each test.**
* **PR5's verdict on 2026-10-21.** Its six conditions are about P&L, the share of fills a book would have accepted,
  orders a day and the weekly gap, and condition 1 compares the frozen replay with the engine. P&L moves by +0.5 % in
  the tightened replay. Condition 1 reads the same stored tape as the engine, so it is untouched. The review should say
  how many paper fills the live orders took; every live fill is recorded with its time and price, so each minute can be
  listed.
* **PR5-R's readings (a) and (b)** count trips and quantities through the price; they should be read with the live
  fills beside them.
* **PR5V and variant-2** replay PR5's stored prints. Their 0.10, 0.20 and 0.30 % rungs sit at PR5's prices, and their
  deeper rungs are reached only after our orders. Not simulated here; the size should be like PR5's.
* **QUEUE** already provides for it: Q3 is reported apart from PR5's first live order, with the account's own fills
  taken out of its tape; Q1 and Q2 read the USD books.
* **PR5-W** studies weekends, when the executor quotes no entries (GBP/USD is dark); only an exit held over a weekend
  rests in the book.

**Recommendation: same price.** The live test exists to measure PR5's own orders (Davies: "测试真实市场吃单情况"), and at
the same price it does. The paper record loses its smallest fills, and its P&L moves by less than any condition's
margin. One tick behind would keep the paper's trips intact in the tightened market, but it costs the live test 20 % of
its fills (85 against 106 in 28 days) and 6 % of its P&L, and it measures orders 1.3 bps away from the strategy's
(11.3 / 21.3 / 31.3 bps instead of 10 / 20 / 30). If Davies prefers the paper's trip counts untouched, one tick behind
is a small executor change (entry and exit ticks moved one tick away from the touch) that this review does not make.

## Inventory

* **What £120 must hold.** Six bids need £60 of GBP (£59.99998 at today's fair). Three asks a book need, in coin,
  39.517 USDC and 39.548 USDT.
* **Bid-only does not supply the asks.** The executor keeps a long's coins for that long's own exit: an ask may sell only
  coin beyond what the book's longs will sell. Bid fills therefore never fund an ask, and the six asks stay skipped for
  good. That is half the rungs unmeasured (in the tightened market the asks were 41 of 102 trips and £0.415 of £1.309 at
  £10).
* **The conversion** (`runQuotesConvert`, `POST ?action=quotes-convert`) buys one book's coin with GBP: an IOC sized at
  the best ask, bounded at fair + 50 bps, at most £30, sent only while the executor is live and armed. At today's books
  (15:38 UTC): USDC/GBP best ask 0.7578, 2.3 bps over fair, 5,000 coins there; USDT/GBP 0.7573, 3.7 bps over fair,
  6,746 coins. **Cost against fair, fee included: £0.034 + £0.038 = £0.072**, about a day and a half of the live
  estimate.
* **The account needs a little more than £120.** The conversion checks free GBP (the balance less every open buy)
  against its size at the fair + 50 bps bound plus the 9 bps fee: £30.17. With six £10 bids resting, the first passes
  (£60 free) and the second does not (£30 free). With £120.20 or more it passes. **Top up to £121**; the executor still
  sizes from `capital_gbp` = 120, so the extra pound only sits there. With £121 the bids also stay funded whether the
  venue takes the buy fee in the coin or in pounds.
* Convert only after the first live turn. Before it, the dry-run's open bids still count as open buys in the check.

## The go-live checklist

**Before.** The change in this review is deployed: a push touching `supabase/functions/agents/` redeploys `agents`
(`edge-functions.yml`); confirm the function's version is newer than the push. Then, read-only:

    -- L1: dry-run, no guards, governor "all"
    select state, updated_at, last_error from public.agent_quote_live_state;
    -- the sub-account (the probe, `only=revx2`): GBP at least 120.20, no USDC or USDT

**1. Capital to 120**, still in dry-run:

    update public.agent_quote_live_config set capital_gbp = 120 where id = 1;

Within a few minutes the dry-run's new bids are about 13.2 coins (£10):

    select ts, book, rung_side, k, price, base_size, round(price * base_size, 4) as gbp, state
    from public.agent_quote_live_orders where mode = 'dry_run' order by id desc limit 12;

**2. Live, on Davies' word in that conversation:**

    update public.agent_quote_live_config set dry_run = false, live_confirmed_at = now() where id = 1;

**3. The first live turn** (within two minutes): six bids, the dry-run's rows cancelled, the asks skipped for want of
coin (`skip` events). Confirm the first live order with Davies there.

    select id, ts, book, rung_side, k, leg, side, price, base_size, state, venue_order_id
    from public.agent_quote_live_orders where mode = 'live' order by id;

**4. The conversion**, one book at a time: first without `send` (it returns the order it would send: base about 39.6 at
the best ask, the bound, the asks' need), then with `"send": true`. The operator's call goes out through pg_net with the
cron bearer, as the minute job's calls do (a call queued by hand waits for the batch in flight):

    select net.http_post(
      url     := 'https://flmvxigozjuizpckllvk.supabase.co/functions/v1/agents?action=quotes-convert',
      headers := jsonb_build_object('Authorization', concat('Bearer ', (select decrypted_secret from vault.decrypted_secrets where name = 'cron_secret')),
                                    'Content-Type', 'application/json'),
      body    := '{"book":"USDC-GBP","gbp":30}'::jsonb,
      timeout_milliseconds := 30000);
    select status_code, content from net._http_response where id = <the id it returned>;

Then the same with `{"book":"USDC-GBP","gbp":30,"send":true}`, and both again for `USDT-GBP`.

**5. The next turn** settles each conversion from the venue's read-back (`leg = 'convert'`: `filled_base` in whole
steps, the fee fields or `feeDerived`, `fromAccount` if the fee was not reported), and the six asks go out. If an ask is
still skipped for a few hundredths of a coin (an ask more than about 11 bps over fair), one more conversion of £0.50 on
that book fills the gap.

**The first hour, against the paper engine:**
* L1 each few minutes: `entryBook` live, `armed` true, no guard on either book, `governor.live` "all", `dayPnlGbp` near 0,
  the balances.
* Every live entry is a paper decision at the paper's ticks (§4 item 35's L3 with `mode = 'live'`: no row).
* No `pending` row older than two minutes; refused rows few (the model expects about 4 refused entries a day).
* `ops_errors` for `agents.quotes_live`: none new.
* After a live fill: its read-back (whole steps, the fee, `fromAccount`), and its exit resting at fair by the next turn.

**The first day:**
* POSTs today (L1 `posts.live`) against the paper engine's orders today: the model expects them within a few percent
  (203 a day against the paper's 205 in the tightened market).
* Live fills against paper fills, decision by decision (`paper_oid`, `paper_live`): a live fill with no paper fill is the
  interaction above (expect a few percent of the paper's fills); a paper fill with no live fill is the 30 s lag, a
  refusal or a fill that was not there.
* The account against the book: GBP plus each coin at fair adds to about £120 plus the realised P&L; the coin held
  equals the longs plus the asks' inventory.
* No `loss_stop` and no `stop_unfilled` event.

**Kill switches.**
* `update public.agent_quote_live_config set live_confirmed_at = null where id = 1;` cancels every live entry and places
  none; exits and the 24-hour stops stay armed.
* `update public.agent_quote_live_config set dry_run = true where id = 1;` goes back to dry-run and winds the live book
  down the same way.
* `agent_risk.global_pause` cancels every open order, exits included, and stops every strategy row with it,
  `trend-4h-live` too: the last resort.

**Never trade by hand in this sub-account (key `_2`).** Its executor books fills and inventory from the account's
balances; a trade by hand would be counted as its own and could be sold by it.

**Left open.** The conversion only buys coin. At the end of the test about £60 of USDC and USDT stays in the account;
turning it back into pounds needs an executor call that does not exist yet, never a trade by hand.

## What this cannot show

* The 1,000-a-day budget's scope (key, sub-account or user) is undocumented.
* The queue ahead of our price is unknown; the model puts our order last at its price, which takes the least flow.
  A real order that joined earlier could take more.
* The interaction on PR5V and variant-2 was not simulated.
* Whether the venue takes a buy's fee in the coin or in pounds has not been read on this account; the plan holds either
  way with £121.
* The conversion's price is one moment's book.
* 28 days of the tightened market, the regime that runs now.
