Ten basis points a side on the one leg. Entries in `[2023-01-01, 2024-01-01)` UTC.

The house bar used for LS-FADE stays as written: both later sub-windows

## Where the fair value is published

The fair-value series is WhiteBIT `GET /api/v1/public/kline` for `BTC_USDT`
with `interval=1d`, from `whitebit.com`. The public page is
`https://docs.whitebit.com/public/kline/`.
Each row is `[time, open, close, high, low, volume, deal]`. `time` is the
candle start in Unix seconds. Every candle stored here starts at 00:00 UTC.
The close is published when the candle ends, at `time + 86400`. A decision at
a Binance entry open uses a WhiteBIT close only when that end is strictly
earlier than the entry. The pull is paged in 90-day chunks. A later year
returned by the endpoint is not stored.

The price being judged is the finished close of the contract being traded.
That close is not the fair value. The open of that contract is the fill and
is not an input. No book ticker is read. Binance spot, the coin-margined
book and the USDT book are not read as this fair value.

## The idea

**WBTCH.** Fair value is the WhiteBIT BTC_USDT daily close. The finished Binance spot close was at least five basis points under that close, so the next Binance spot open is a price that finished cheap to WhiteBIT. Buy Binance spot for sixteen days. The other trade buys Binance spot for sixteen days on these same prices when that discount is absent. Same side. The edge is turned off. Not the opposite side. Not an unconditional hold. The entry open is not an input. One leg.

Zero basis points left the cheap set longer than the edge-off set (176 against 174). Five basis points is the first line where the edge-off set is longer: 168 against 182. The rich side of this WhiteBIT premium is not a second rule, and a wider-basis rule is not shipped.
