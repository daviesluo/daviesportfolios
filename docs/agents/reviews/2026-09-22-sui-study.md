## 1. Why the four-window rule cannot see SUI

The repository ranks a coin-set change by its **worst** of four windows. SUI's
history starts 2023-05-03: window C's in-sample would be 142 days, under the
180-day floor, and D's in-sample — the two years to 2022-09-22 — ends seven
months before SUI's tape begins. So C and D are **four-coin sleeves with or
without SUI**, the live row's worst window is D in every condition, and
`drop·SUI` reports **0.00 in all four conditions — recomputed here, 0 / 0 / 0 /
0** (`claimsChecked.dropSuiDeltaIsAnIdentity`). That zero is an identity. Every
method below exists to judge SUI where the evidence can see it, and to judge the
other four members the same way so SUI is not held to a different standard.

## 4. The primary test — SUI's rank among the five, fold by fold

**Written before any number was computed, and not changed after:** tile SUI's
whole history with consecutive folds, run every coin from flat in every fold,
and ask for each of the five members what removing it does to the sleeve's
ret/DD at the same capital. Under "SUI's seat is like any other member's", SUI's
rank in each fold is uniform on 1…5; the null of the rank sum is enumerated
exactly. **p(worse) < 0.05 on both tapes → drop; p(better) < 0.05 on both → the
seat is supported; otherwise the performance evidence cannot decide.**

The span: 2023-05-20 → 2026-09-20, 3.34 years, where the seeded rule can decide
on all five. **Seven folds of 174 days** — the whole number of folds nearest to
six-month ones, so neither SUI's listing months nor the most recent ones are
dropped.

Shipped stop, Coinbase tape (rank 1 = the member the sleeve can least afford
to lose):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2023-05-20 | 2.44 | 3.58 | +1.14 | −8.28 % | 2 | 5 / 5 / 4 |
| 2 | 2023-11-10 | 2.90 | 2.79 | −0.11 | +7.25 % | 2 | 3 / 4 / 1 |
| 3 | 2024-05-02 | −0.10 | 0.19 | +0.29 | −11.16 % | 4 | 4 / 4 / 3 |
| 4 | 2024-10-23 | 1.93 | 1.98 | +0.05 | 0.00 % | 0 | 4 / 5 / 1 |
| 5 | 2025-04-15 | 1.08 | 1.20 | +0.12 | +0.21 % | 10 | 4 / 4 / 2 |
| 6 | 2025-10-06 | **−0.85** | **−1.00** | −0.15 | +3.15 % | 4 | **1 / 1 / 1** |
| 7 | 2026-03-30 | 1.85 | 2.26 | +0.41 | +2.98 % | 8 | 4 / 4 / 5 |

Kraken's tape (the one `signal_venue` makes the loop read) tells the same story

Under the retired `trail` stop the primary mean rank is exactly **3.00** on both

**Reading.** SUI is a slightly worse earner than the other four and a slightly
better damper, fold after fold. On the risk-adjusted measure the balance tilts
slightly against it — dropping it raises ret/DD in 5 of 7 folds on both tapes —
but the primary test sits well inside chance on both. The one place SUI is

were the 3.5 seen here, a one-sided test at 5 % with 80 % power needs about **50
folds — roughly 24 years** of 174-day folds (`verdict.power`). Neither the
backtest (SUI has 3.3 years) nor the forward record (two folds a year) will get
there.

## 10. Paper and live are different bars — and what would decide it

**PAPER — keep.** §4.15's bar admits new coins; it does not certify the ones
already in, and **SUI would not be admitted today**: under the running stop it
clears window A only, on both tapes, seeded and chosen — and §6 shows its
original admission rested on 36 hours. The precedent is AVAX, which failed the
tightened bar the morning it was added and was kept in paper "because paper is
free and the record is the test". The reason to keep SUI in paper is not its
return record, which would take decades to read (§4). It is the two things only
the paper row measures and that SUI needs more than any other member:

- **the book it actually meets** — `bookBps` on every decision row. Its sampled
  book runs from one tick to 38.6 bps; the backtests charge 23.94.
- **what the model actually says at its entries** — two-thirds to three-quarters
  of SUI's entries are high-volatility states, where its measured replies veto
  2 and 3 calls in 5: under the gate, the least predictable member of the row.

If SUI is in the live row, the paper `trend-4h` row is also its control (the
go-live draft keeps it as one) and must hold it anyway.

**LIVE — keep, as the incumbent.** The live bar is higher, and the question it
asks is whether the evidence says the sleeve is better **without** SUI. It does
not: the pre-registered test is undecided, the secondary tests split by
direction, and the repository's own worst-period rule — the ranking every
settled live decision rests on — says removing SUI makes the worst period worse
wherever it can see SUI under the running stop (the worst fold on both tapes,
the bear window on all three). A change needs evidence and this is not evidence.
Dropping it is equally defensible only if the owner wants the smaller row for
its own sake: the numbers cannot tell the two apart (four × $20 with $20 idle
differs from five × $20 by $1.17 / $0.21 over 3.3 years and by $1.27 / $1.98
over SUI's two out-of-sample years), and four × $25 needs `max_order_usd`
raised.

**What would decide it, and how long.**

- **Performance cannot, on any useful horizon.** The same test would need about
  50 folds of 174 days — about **24 years** — to detect an effect of the size
  seen, if it were real; the backtest has 7 and the forward record adds two a
  year. The return side alone is nearer — dropping SUI raises the return per
  dollar in 6 of 7 folds, t-test p = 0.049 / 0.051 — but return is half of the
  trade the seat makes: it buys damping with return, and the pre-registered
  test prices both. By the rule written before the run, a secondary result
  never becomes the verdict.
- **The gate decides how much the seat matters, and that is the jev study's
  question, not this one's.** The model has already been asked every entry
  state five times (reference §4.21: 450 calls at 18:29 UTC, `jev_answers.json`). With the gate as it runs, about half of SUI's
  entries would be refused (7.8 of 15 on the Coinbase tape, 9.0 of 16 on Kraken's) and its slot would sit in cash more than
  any other member's; with the model in shadow, as the jev study recommends for
  the live row and its control, nothing is refused and §4–§7 are the live
  numbers. Either way the seat question stays where §4 left it.
- **The paper row's `bookBps`** measures SUI's real cost at its decisions — at
  about 4.5 round trips a year, a year of paper gives a handful of observations.
  §7 already shows the cost does not decide the seat at any measured width.

So the honest answer to "is it still worth including?" is that **the evidence
cannot decide, and at the effect size seen the pre-registered test would need
about 24 years of folds to decide it**.
Keeping it is the default because removal is the change that would need the
evidence.

## 11. What this could not settle

1. **The model is not priced here.** Every return is the rulebook's. §8 applies
   the model's measured replies to the entries the rulebook takes — expected
   refusals per coin — but does not re-run the sleeve with them; the jev study
   does (`jev.json`, `perCoinPrimary`). The replies are today's model asked
   about states from 2023 onwards.
2. **Seven folds are seven.** Consecutive and non-overlapping, but the same five
   coins in one market, so a fold is not a fresh draw and the rank null's
   independence across folds is an approximation.
3. **Window A's calendar differs by coin by up to 36 hours**, in every published
   table. §6 measures what that does; it does not redefine the windows.
4. **The book was sampled for half an hour on one evening.** A week of sampling
   would pin SUI's distribution; it would not change §7's conclusion, because
   the spread does not decide the seat at any measured width.
5. **Signal and fill are on one series** in every arm here, as in set2; the loop
   decides on Kraken and fills on Revolut X (`backtest_fill.ts` owns that split).
6. **The live stop is judged at the bid**, every minute. SUI's UK book is the
   widest of the five, §6 shows SUI's window-A result turning on one bar's low
   that differed by tape, and the UK tape prints lows the other two do not (0.83
   against 0.871 on 2026-09-20 20:00; 0.941 against 1.0128 / 1.0055 on 09-21
   12:00). A floor judged at the bid could fire on a wick no backtest bar
   contains. Not measurable without the live record.
7. **`agent_risk`'s daily loss limit is not simulated**, as in every study before.
