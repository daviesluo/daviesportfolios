PMPOST: why paper and the simulators overstated live, quantified. Live figures from results/decompose.txt and
results/phase_boot.txt; the rest quoted from the studies named (their committed outputs).

1. S2 (live-prep's busy books, 10-09 01:32 -> 10-12 07:30), against its own paper layer (LP-ADVERSE §4)
   paper layer on its own record: +$91.2 a quoting day (rewards $100.1 at R 0.75, fills to the end -$8.9)
   live: -$21.45 a quoting day (this study: rewards 46.06, rebates 12.96, fills -128.68 over 3.249 days)
   the gap, about $112 a day: rewards $85 (76 %), fills $31, rebates -$4.
   a. R (paid over the path's own formula): S2 46.06 / 64.76 = 0.711, Q2 3.76 / 5.26 = 0.715, against the 0.75 paper
      assumed: about 5 % of the rewards gap. R was never the problem.
   b. Formula realised per filled share: paper 7.7 c, live 0.93 c (S2: 64.76 / 6,986), 8.3x lower. Why:
      - carried stock held the cap: 39.6 % of selected quoting minutes rested nothing (LP-ADVERSE), 68 % of live's
        market-minutes were carried markets working exits;
      - two-sided scoring minutes 1,833 a day against paper's 7,920, each worth $0.0103 against $0.0162;
      - one side or neither read scoring in 16.7 % more; programme cuts after 10-09 only 3 % (0.97x).
   c. Fill model: paper filled only on a print strictly through the quote and saw 0.077 shares a quoted minute against
      live's 0.140 (1.8x). The fills it saw were the adverse half: live fills with a print within 60 s lost 4.86 c a share
      at 60 minutes, the other 234 lost 0.78 c. So per share paper and live lost the same (-2.92 c against -2.85 c);
      paper missed volume, not toxicity.
   d. Queue position: improving the touch by a tick brought no extra takers, k = 0.81 [0.41, 2.25] (LP-BEST §1).
      Not a source of the gap.
   e. Informed takers: 97 % of the 60-minute markout came from one-shot takers (a sweep, or a single taker at our price
      with no print before), 122 + 254 fills (LP-SPEED). Paper's per-share markout already contained them.
   Against LP-BEST's calibrated engine (R 0.420 in its units, fills x k_vol 1.283), S2 is reproduced: engine -$24.96 a
   quoting day, live -$21.77 (out of sample in time -78.02 against -75.13). Calibrated to busy books, the engine is right
   about busy books.

2. Q2 (quiet books, 10-13 13:25 -> 10-15 00:08), against LP-BEST's calibrated engine (its §4, the whole span):
   engine: +$3.62 a day [+1.38, +6.02]: rewards 3.97, fills -0.58, rebates 0.23; 136 filled shares a day
   live:   -$10.89 a day [-19.96, -2.61]: rewards 2.60, fills -14.43, rebates 0.92; 510 filled shares a day
   gap -$14.51 a day = rewards -1.37 + fills -13.85 + rebates +0.69. The fills' -13.85 splits into
     volume (510 against 136 shares a day at the engine's -0.43 c a share): -1.6
       - x1.8 because pm-rec recorded the picked books about 56 % of each day and the engine quotes only those minutes
         (LP-BEST disclosed it as an upside: it doubles fills as well as rewards);
       - x1.9 from Addendum 13's refills while they ran (Q2-PM: 174 -> 332 shares on the same window);
     per share (live -2.83 c against the engine's -0.43 c): -12.2 — the takers of quiet books were informed over hours:
       Q2's 16 lots: entry edge +5.87, first minute -9.12, drift while held -16.08, exits +3.00 (Q2-TRIPS), 15 of 16 lots
       lost (P = 0.00026 under a coin), and the engine's tape rule charged -1.96 c a share where live lost -2.88 c to the
       exits (Q2-TRIPS §2: the calibration's extra -0.918 c a share).
   rewards per filled share: engine 2.92 c, live 0.51 c (5.7x lower): volume up, and the rewards themselves x0.53-0.57
   (x2's pause tripping 13x as often on flickering quiet books, 380 against 29 minutes; rate dips; refills).
   The busy-book calibration did not carry over to quiet books.

3. Lessons for any future Polymarket test (each one cost money here):
   L1. The measure is rewards per filled share against the adverse cost per filled share, from live-like fills. Formula
       income is never the measurement. S2 needed 1.66 c a share and was paid 0.66 c; Q2 needed 2.65 c and was paid 0.51 c.
   L2. Fills come from the trade tape of maker orders (a taker who takes exactly our price leaves no print through it),
       and fill volume and markout are calibrated on live orders before any arm is believed.
   L3. Mark at 1 minute, 60 minutes, 4 hours and to the exit or the payout; quiet books' information arrives over hours.
   L4. Simulate the path's own mechanics: refills, the pause on the real (flickering) mids, the cap held by carried
       stock, programme churn read every minute, and the hours the recorder missed.
   L5. A calibration fitted in one regime does not transfer to another: re-fit per regime before money.
   L6. Read results by market and lot with a cluster bootstrap and a sign count; one market was 25 % of S2's loss.
   L7. Thresholds chosen on the days that judge them are flattered; freeze and read unseen days (DEADLINE-NO's path).
   L8. Anything whose edge needs sub-second reaction is out of reach (LP-SPEED, PMSC).
   L9. No paper layer measures R or live fill quality; only a minimum-size live pilot does, and it needs a stop that
       allows for the day's worst informed fills (a −$25 stop had $3.98 of room after 28 hours).
