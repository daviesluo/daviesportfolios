EXPENSIVE-LIMIT (2026-10-09): the best limit on live-prep's buys of near-certain tokens, as a share of its capital
=================================================================================================================
Asked by Davies, 2026-10-09, verbatim: "加上，但你研究下这个最多买的数值最优的设定后再加，并且以持仓比例来算不是硬数值". In English:
add it, but first research the best setting for how much it may buy, and make it a proportion of the holdings/capital,
not a fixed dollar figure. "It" is EXPENSIVE-SIDE's recommendation (results/expensive_side.txt, commit 7aaaf0f6): a cap on
live-prep BUYING a token priced >= 0.95, there "$25 at cost a market". Not blind: every record below had been read before.

THE FORM, AND WHY
- A BUY of a token whose buy price is >= thr rests only while that token's holding in the market AT THE MINUTE'S MARK plus
  the order at its price stays <= f x C, with C the path's capital: its total cap as the order path reads it each turn
  (lpLimits' capTotal: pm_lp_config.cap_total_usd under the code's ceiling, $320 today). Sells are never limited.
- Of C, not of the per-market cap: the per-market cap has a code ceiling of $100 that does not move with the account, and
  what a single loss means is its size against the whole account (and against the -$75 stop's room). f x C reads "no
  market's near-certain buys can put more than f of the capital at risk" and grows with C whatever raises it (reinvested
  rewards, deposits).
- At the mark, not at cost: what can still be lost is what the holding is worth now, and the rule has the mark (RW's
  adjusted mid) on every turn without the fills' cost. (At cost was swept too; it is no better, section 2.)
- Shares as a multiple of N do not scale with capital (N is the market's reward minimum, <= 20), and a dollar figure does
  not either; a share of C does both jobs.

THE OBJECTIVE (per 30 days, R = 0.40, each f against L1 = no limit)
  U(lambda, rate) = E[P&L] - (1 + lambda) x E[near-certain loss at the live speed] - lambda x E[loss from single events
  >= 5 % of C on the simulated holdings]
  E[P&L] = the mean of five records: the four simulated cells and the paper replay. lambda = 1 counts a dollar lost in one
  hit twice, as the loss and as the room it takes of the -$75 total stop that halts the whole path (the source of the
  rewards); lambda = 0 is risk-neutral and 3 risk-averse. The rate: the calibration's 0.97-0.99 favourites with 3-14 days
  left, 1.39 % per 7-day holding pooled by hour [0.99, 1.87], 3.35 % with each market weighted once, 2.65 % for 0.95-0.99.
  Neither lambda nor the true rate is known, and the per-scenario optimum moves with them (0.125 at the pooled rate and
  lambda <= 1; 0.10 at lambda 3 or the per-market rate; 0.08 / 0.0625 at the per-market rate and lambda 1 / 3). So the
  choice is the f whose largest shortfall from the best arm over all nine scenarios is smallest (minimax regret). That rule
  was picked after seeing that the scenario optima disagree; it was not written down before the runs.

ANSWER: f = 8 % of capital, at 0.95 ($25.60 at $320). Its largest shortfall from the best arm in any scenario is $1.85 a
month (0.10: $2.15; 0.125: $5.50; 0.0625: $4.82; 0.15: $9.70). No limit (L1) is $8.83 / $13.11 / $21.68 a month behind
8 % at the pooled rate and lambda 0 / 1 / 3.
- Reward cost at C = $320, R = 0.40, against L1 (boot = index 100 of 2,000 paired day draws, P(> 0)):
    RW strict -1.72 (boot -7.65, P 0.31); RW at-price +1.36 (-5.66, 0.65); full universe strict +2.24 (-0.03, 0.68),
    at-price +2.19 (-0.05, 0.68); paper replay: 494 minutes stopped, $3.09 of $152.74 near-certain formula reward in 4.3 days
    (-$8.59 a month at R = 0.40), the one place 8 % visibly costs (partial fills of 7-19 shares leave the market one-sided:
    see the caveat). At 10 % the paper loses $0.20; the simulations cannot tell 8 % and 10 % apart.
- What it bounds: a market's near-certain buys stop at $25.60 at the mark, 8 % of capital and 34 % of the stop's room,
  where today one market reached $78 (24 %) in 21 minutes and the old cap allowed $97 (30 %). At the live speed the
  expected near-certain loss falls from $5.41 to $1.43 a month (pooled rate), and the loss when it comes from 30 % of C
  to at most 8 %.
- The threshold: 0.95. At 8 %, 0.93 costs more on RW (-2.11 / -5.00) and the paper (-$3.17 formula), and 0.97 costs
  -$10.38 / -$7.68 on RW (it lets 0.95-0.97 buys through, whose holdings then block the >= 0.97 buys of the same token);
  0.95 is ahead of both at every f >= 6.25 % in section 7.
- At larger capital: 8 % costs nothing measurable at $640, $1,000 or $2,000 (largest cost -1.28, pr at-price, $640 scaled)
  and stops binding at about $1,250 (8 % of C passes the $100 a market cap, which takes over). Below about $240 to $250
  (8 % x C under 20 x 0.95-0.99) no first order of N = 20 fits and the near-certain side rests nothing (as "no buy", -$7.49 on RW
  strict): the share follows the capital down too.

CONFIDENCE: moderate on the band, low on the exact point. Every f from 8 % to 12.5 % is within $5.50 a month of the best
in all nine scenarios and within $2 at the pooled rate; the differences are smaller than the bootstrap's spread (index 100
at -$3 to -$8 over 14 days). What the choice rests on: a calibration from a year of other markets (not live-prep's own),
13 hours of live fills on one market for the speed, and a paper replay that is first order. Caveat: a partial fill of
7-19 shares of a 20-share near-certain bid leaves that side unable to rest (neither the buy, over the limit, nor a sell,
under N) until the price leaves 0.95+, the holding is sold off as a carried market, or the day's reselection; the paper
replay counts that cost, the live path may meet it more often than its paper did.
