4. Recommendation: adopt a $25 cap (at cost, per market) on BUYING a token priced >= 0.95, sells of held tokens unchanged;
   equivalently close to "inventory 1N at >= 0.95" (which allows up to ~2N - 1 = 39 shares, ~$38, after a partial fill).
   It costs nothing measurable (-1.72 / +1.36 / +2.24 / +2.19 at R = 0.40 over the four cells; every bootstrap index 100
   between -7.7 and 0.0) and bounds what one direct expensive buy can lose to ~$25 (7.8 % of $320, a third of the stop)
   instead of ~$97. Do not extend it to 0.90: every 0.90 rule loses $6-$63 on RW's record strict (P>0 <= 0.06) and $2-$51 at-price,
   and the 1N / $25 caps lose $27 on the full universe strict, even though 0.90-0.95 favourites are slightly overpriced. Do not use "no buy" or "skip": they
   give up the reward of the near-certain markets (-$7 to -$17 at 0.95, R = 0.40, RW strict).
   Confidence: moderate. The cost side is consistent across both records and both fill models but small and noisy; the
   benefit side rests on the calibration (a year of public history, not live-prep's own universe, hourly prices) and on
   the live path filling the expensive side far faster than the paper does, which the simulation cannot show. Not
   addressed by any buy rule: tokens bought cheaper that rise to >= 0.95 (the simulation's largest single holdings,
   $107-$115 at the mark); and the 80 NO already held stay held (the rule would only stop further buys there).
